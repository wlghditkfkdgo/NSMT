# SNN / neuromorphic ECG heartbeat classification on MIT-BIH: evaluation split and use of neighbouring-beat context

Written 2026-11-26. Scope: spiking neural network (SNN) and neuromorphic-hardware papers 2017–2026 that classify MIT-BIH heartbeats (plus a few that report the Kachuee 2018 187-sample Kaggle version of MIT-BIH).

Terms used below:
- **Random beat split / intra-patient**: all beats from (nearly) all records are pooled and split at random, so the same patients appear in training and test.
- **Inter-patient (DS1/DS2)**: the de Chazal / AAMI split. Train on the 22 DS1 records, test on the 22 DS2 records; no test patient is seen in training.
- **Patient-specific**: a model is trained or calibrated on part of the test patient's own recording (e.g. Kiranyaz protocol: common DS1 beats + first 5 min of each DS2 patient; or a per-record random split).
- **Neighbouring-beat context**: RR intervals (time to the previous / next R-peak), multi-beat windows, or a continuous ECG stream.

Search route: Crossref and arXiv keyword searches (OpenAlex daily budget was exhausted on this server), Semantic Scholar batch abstracts, Europe PMC full texts, arXiv / repository PDFs, web search. MDPI and ScienceDirect pages returned 403 / bot checks, so some numbers come from abstracts or from secondary sources; this is flagged per item.

## Q1. Which SNN / neuromorphic ECG papers 2017–2026 use MIT-BIH, with which split, input, classes and metrics?

### Takeaway
Most SNN MIT-BIH papers evaluate on a random beat split or a patient-specific protocol and classify one isolated beat (a fixed window around the R-peak) with no RR information. The headline 97–99 % accuracies come from those protocols. Hardware papers sometimes mislabel which protocol a cited work used, so protocol labels have to be checked in the original paper.

**Protocol quotes and other details**
- Amirshahi & Hashemi (patient-specific): "the following patients from the DS2 group are used as test data: 200, 201, … 234 … To train the model for each of the above patients, two sets of data are combined, in specific, randomly selected representative heartbeats from all arrhythmia classes in DS1, plus the first five minutes of the patient's own ECG signal." Energy: "the energy consumption is 1.78 µJ … per beat" — [arXiv 1905.02954](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02954)
- Choi et al. review classes Amirshahi as "patient-specific paradigm" (97.9 %, 4 classes), Yan as "inter-patient paradigm" (90 %, 4 classes), and Xing (98.2 %, 5 classes), Buettner (97.8 %, 5 classes) and Feng (84.4 %, PhysioNet 2017, not MIT-BIH) as "trained with randomly selected data" — [Choi et al., Biomed. - Bauer et al.: "Three disjoint subsets of the input data were used for training, validation and testing … For the training set, recordings were segmented into individual heartbeats. For validation and test sets, recordings were split into longer segments, each comprising five to ten contiguous heartbeats of the same label … segments were then selected at random and concatenated." ### Inferences
- Of the roughly 25 MIT-BIH SNN papers checked, the protocol breaks down as: random beat split (Buettner, Chen, Scrugli, SparrowSNN, Diware, Xing main result, Rana 70:30, Kachuee-set papers), patient-specific (Amirshahi, Liu, Zhao, Banjo & Ghoraani main result, Mao after on-chip learning), inter-patient (Q2 list), and undetermined (Chu 2021/2022, Corradi, several 2024–2026 hardware abstracts).
- The 97–99 % SNN accuracies used as "state of the art" in hardware comparison tables (Chu 98.22, Mao 97.36, SparrowSNN 98.61, Scrugli 98.4, Xing 98.26) are random or patient-specific numbers. Under inter-patient the same groups report 86–93 % (Q2).
- Rana & Kim's 500-sample window (±250 samples at 360 Hz = ±0.69 s) is longer than one RR interval at heart rates above about 87 bpm, so at those rates the window contains part of the neighbouring beats. The authors do not present this as rhythm context (my reading).

## Q2. How many SNN papers use the inter-patient split, which of them use RR or neighbouring-beat context, and what are the best S-class and V-class numbers?

### Takeaway
I found five peer-reviewed SNN papers that report MIT-BIH inter-patient (DS1/DS2) results plus one non-peer-reviewed one. Only two of them use RR information (Banjo & Ghoraani 2026, as four hand-computed RR features; Mishra 2026, as one of 11 features), and none feeds multi-beat windows or a beat sequence. Reported inter-patient SNN numbers are weak for S: the best verified-ish value is Yan 2021's S sensitivity 67 % at +P 46 %. For V the best is V F1 0.804 (Banjo & Ghoraani, with RR features); Xing reports V Sen 69.04 / +P 76.46, Yan V Sen 77 / +P 59.

### Inferences
- Every inter-patient SNN result is from a single-beat classifier. RR enters only as hand-computed scalar features appended at the classifier head (Banjo & Ghoraani) or as one of several Pan-Tompkins features (Mishra). No SNN paper lets the network itself see the previous beats (multi-beat window, beat sequence, or recurrent state carried across beats) under the inter-patient protocol.
- Under inter-patient, adding four RR features to an SNN gave about +6 pp accuracy and +0.07 macro-F1 (Banjo & Ghoraani). This is the only SNN measurement of the value of beat context under DS1/DS2 that I found, and it is an exploratory section of a paper whose main protocol is patient-specific.
- The published inter-patient SNN S-class numbers (best: Sen 67 / +P 46) are well below the non-spiking RR-using CNN in the same review table (S F1 69–82, V F1 93–95). A new SNN that reports per-class S and V under DS1/DS2 would face a thin, weak SNN baseline set; the relevant competition is the non-spiking inter-patient literature.
- Accuracy alone is uninformative here: Mishra's ~0.89 accuracy with ~0.31 macro-recall shows a model that mostly predicts N, because MIT-BIH is about 89–90 % N beats (Chen et al. count 90,081 N of 100,687 AAMI-labelled beats in the whole database — [arXiv 2205.13292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13292); Scrugli et al.: "roughly 89.5%" — [DATE 2024](https://past.date-conference.com/proceedings-archive/2024/DATA/950_pdf_upload.pdf)). The DS2-only share was not checked.

### Gaps
- Per-class S/V numbers for Galvis-Chacón 2026 (Neurocomputing; the only non-MDPI peer-reviewed inter-patient SNN besides Yan) and the SVEB number for Banjo & Ghoraani's inter-patient run (likely in their supplementary Tables S1/S2, not read).
- Yan 2021's own tables were not read; its per-class values come from two secondary tables that agree on N and V, and only one gives S.
- No SNN paper was found that reports macro-F1 under inter-patient apart from Banjo & Ghoraani (no absolute value captured) and Mishra (macro-recall only).

## Q3. Do any SNN papers process the ECG as a continuous stream (beats in sequence) rather than isolated beats?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only in neuromorphic-hardware anomaly-detection or reservoir papers (Bauer 2019, Corradi 2019, Liu 2019, Payvand 2023), all with random, single-record or patient-specific splits and mostly binary or coarse labels. The level-crossing / delta-modulation papers (Chu 2022, Scrugli 2024, Zhao 2026) turn the signal into an event stream but still classify one segmented beat at a time. None evaluates a continuous-stream SNN under the inter-patient protocol.

### Cited Findings
- Bauer et al. (TBioCAS 2019): "Multi-channel analog ECG traces are encoded as asynchronous streams of binary events and processed using a spiking recurrent neural network operated in a reservoir computing paradigm"; test input = randomly selected segments of "five to ten contiguous heartbeats of the same label" concatenated; training input = 30,000 randomly drawn single beats concatenated — [arXiv 1911.05521](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05521)
- Corradi et al. (IJCNN 2019): "compressing ECG signals into a stream of asynchronous digital events that are used to stimulate the recurrent SNN"; "Using ablative analysis, we demonstrate the impact of the recurrent SNN" — [DOI 10.1109/IJCNN.2019.8852279](https://doi.org/10.1109/IJCNN.2019.8852279) (abstract only)
- Liu (2019 thesis): PVC detection "as a supervised temporal classification problem which demands a binary output signal y(n) for each heartbeat", per-subject 10 min train / next 5 min test on DYNAP-SE — [arXiv 1905.12116](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12116)
- Payvand et al. (ICONS 2023): delta-modulated raw recording of patient 208 into dendritic delays; "The average heartbeat duration is on the order of 700 ms, so the relevant temporal features should be a fraction of that period" (delays of ~40 ms, i.e. within-beat features) — [arXiv 2305.06941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06941)
- Gerber et al. (BioCAS 2022): on-chip delay chains act as "a working memory with the longest duration possible", but the inputs are Kachuee's isolated beat segments "padded with zeros to make them equal length" — [arXiv 2209.01266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.01266)
- Event-stream but per-beat: Chu 2022 (LC sampling "directly fed into an SNN in an event-driven manner", 5-class per beat) — [DOI 10.1109/TBCAS.2022.3189364](https://doi.org/10.1109/TBCAS.2022.3189364); Scrugli 2024 (delta-modulated segmented beats, random split) — [DATE 2024](https://past.date-conference.com/proceedings-archive/2024/DATA/950_pdf_upload.pdf); Zhao 2026 (time-aware LIF that incorporates "event intervals into membrane dynamics", beat-level input) — [York thesis](https://yorkspace.library.yorku.ca/items/606f08b1-f591-40cb-9568-116baaca373d)
- Zhao's thesis notes for its non-spiking baseline that S beats "are often relatively similar to normal beats in local morphology, while their more distinctive characteristics are usually reflected in rhythm relationships, such as premature occurrence and changes in the RR-interval" — [York thesis](https://yorkspace.library.yorku.ca/items/606f08b1-f591-40cb-9568-116baaca373d)
- Outside MIT-BIH, per-time-step ECG wave labelling on QTDB is the standard streaming ECG task in SNN neuron papers (one level-crossing sample per step, label per step) — [Yin et al. 2021, arXiv 2103.12593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.12593)

### Inferences
- Bauer's training stream concatenates randomly drawn single beats, so the rhythm the reservoir sees during training is artificial; any cross-beat information there is not real RR context (my reading of the quoted procedure).
- A spiking model that reads MIT-BIH as a beat sequence (or a multi-beat window) and is tested under DS1/DS2 with per-class S/V reporting would have no direct SNN precedent among the papers found. The nearest SNN data point for "context helps across patients" is Banjo & Ghoraani's +6.2 pp from four RR scalars.

### Gaps
- Corradi 2019's split and how its event stream is cut into beats could not be read (IEEE full text unavailable; ETH record metadata-only).
- I did not search non-English venues or patents beyond one hit (EP 4386622 A1, "SNN-based ECG classifier for wearable edge devices", not read — [EPO](https://data.epo.org/gpi/EP4386622A1)).
