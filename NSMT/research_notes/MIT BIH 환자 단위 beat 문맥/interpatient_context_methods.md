# MIT-BIH inter-patient (DS1 -> DS2) heartbeat classification that uses information from other beats: methods, ablations, validity concerns

Scope note (applies to all sections). "Inter-patient" = de Chazal et al. 2004 split, DS1 (22 records) train / DS2 (22 records) test, 4 paced records excluded, AAMI classes N/S/V/F/Q. "Context" = anything taken from beats other than the current one: RR intervals (pre-RR = previous interval, post-RR = next interval, local / average RR), raw waveforms of neighbouring beats, beat sequences, whole segments. "Causal" = uses only the current and earlier beats. Numbers are copied from the paper itself unless marked "secondary" (copied from another paper's comparison table) or "abstract only". Reliability labels: [J] = established journal / IEEE conference; [Hindawi], [MDPI], [preprint], [GitHub] = lower reliability. Citation counts are Crossref "is-referenced-by" counts queried on 2026-11-26 (OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar were rate-limited from this server, so their counts were not available).

## Q1. Is "inter-patient split + information from preceding / neighbouring beats" an established method, or does the literature mostly use plain single beats?

### Takeaway
Using information from other beats under the inter-patient split is the default, not a niche: the paper that defined the split (de Chazal 2004) already fed RR-interval features (pre-RR, post-RR, local and average RR) to its classifier, and most later inter-patient methods, classical and deep, keep scalar RR features. Feeding raw waveforms of several beats (multi-beat windows, beat sequences, 10-s segments) is a smaller line that starts in 2018-2019 (Guo et al., Mousavi & Afghah, Sellami & Hwang). Strictly past-only (causal) context is rare: almost all methods use the post-RR interval, record-wide RR averages, bidirectional RNNs or whole segments, i.e. future beats.

### Cited Findings
- Origin of the protocol already uses inter-beat features: de Chazal, O'Dwyer & Reilly, IEEE TBME 51(7):1196-1206, 2004 (DOI 10.1109/TBME.2004.827359) split 44 non-paced records into two sets of 22 records (~50,000 beats each); "Twelve configurations ... were compared. Feature sets were based on ECG morphology, heartbeat intervals, and RR-intervals"; selected configuration on the second set: SVEB Se 75.9 %, +P 38.5 %, FPR 4.7 %; VEB Se 77.7 %, +P 81.9 %, FPR 1.2 % (abstract) — [PubMed 15248536](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15248536/). ### Inferences
- Groups, from the ~35 inter-patient learned methods I could characterise (full text, abstract or secondary): about 28-30 use information from other beats (mostly scalar RR features; about 10 feed raw neighbouring waveforms or sequences), about 5 are single-beat only. The PubMed abstract count (≈20/63) is a lower bound: e.g. Zhang J. 2021 uses RR ratios in its full text but its PubMed abstract did not match any context term, and many key venues (ESWA, BSPC, Biocybernetics, Knowledge-Based Systems, IEEE Access) are not indexed in PubMed.
- "Inter-patient + preceding-beat input" is therefore not novel by itself. What is uncommon: (a) raw multi-beat input that is strictly causal, (b) long past context (tens of beats), (c) any test of whether the ORDER of the past beats matters (none found, see Q2).

### Gaps
- A complete count over all venues was not possible (OpenAlex daily budget exhausted, Semantic Scholar rate-limited); the counts above are a PubMed sample plus a curated list.
- de Chazal 2004 per-configuration results (12 feature-set configurations, i.e. with / without RR sets) were not read; only the abstract was available.

## Q2. Measured gain from inter-beat context in ablations (especially S class), with exact numbers

### Takeaway
Every ablation found shows a gain for the S class when inter-beat information is added, from small (+1.8 pp Se) to very large (S Se 6.3 % -> 78.8 %). The large gains come from lower-reliability journals (Hindawi J Healthc Eng) and the RR features involved partly use the whole test record or the next beat. No paper was found that shuffles beat order or RR order, so "more beats / RR values" has never been separated from "ordered past".

### Cited Findings
- Zhang J., Liu A., Liang D., Chen X., Gao M., J Healthc Eng 2021 [Hindawi], DOI 10.1155/2021/9946596. Inputs: 2-lead beat (128 samples x 2) + "pre-RR ratio (the ratio of the current pre-RR interval to the average of all pre-RR intervals of the corresponding recording)" + "near-pre-RR ratio (... to the average of the previous ten pre-RR intervals)". Table 5, DS2: without RR ratios Acc 94.0, Sen_N 98.7 %, Pre_N 95.0 %, Sen_S 6.3 %, Pre_S 42.2 %, Sen_V 88.7 %, Pre_V 91.3 %; with RR ratios Acc 94.7, Sen_N 96.2 %, Pre_N 98.0 %, Sen_S 78.8 %, Pre_S 90.8 %, Sen_V 92.5 %, Pre_V 94.3 %. Final F1_S 84.4 %, F1_V 93.4 % — [PMC8181174](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8181174/)
- Wu H. et al., J Healthc Eng 2022 [Hindawi], DOI 10.1155/2022/9370517: window-length ablation (my reading: at MIT-BIH's 360 Hz, 508 samples ≈ ±0.7 s around the R peak, reaching toward the previous and next beats; the paper does not state the sampling rate or relate the window to neighbouring beats). 508-sample window = "250 samples before and 257 samples after the R-peak" vs conventional 256-sample window. Table 9: Signal-256 + focal loss + ensemble: Acc 89.52, S Sen 16.34, S +P 14.60, V Sen 85.15, V +P 77.32; Signal-508 + focal loss + ensemble: Acc 91.89, S Sen 80.23, S +P 49.40, V Sen 90.99, V +P 83.09. Authors: "The medical diagnosis of arrhythmia is not only based on a single heartbeat but is determined by combining multiple consecutive heartbeats" — [PMC9481402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9481402/)
- iScience 2024 (Cell Press) multi-branch CNN + Transformer, RR = pre-RR, post-RR, pre/post ratio; Table 7 RR ablation (%). EXP2 (DS1 train, DS2 test, but "the first 5 min of each record from the test set were added to the training set"): without RR VEB Sen 97.7, Ppr 96.4, F1 97.0; SVEB Sen 77.4, Ppr 87.2, F1 82.0; overall 98.4 -> with RR VEB Sen 97.6, Ppr 98.0, F1 97.6; SVEB Sen 81.5, Ppr 94.9, F1 87.7; overall 98.8. EXP3 (20-record "DS100" train / 24-record "DS200" test): without RR VEB Sen 96.0, Ppr 92.6, F1 94.3; SVEB Sen 63.0, Ppr 82.4, F1 71.4; overall 96.8 -> with RR VEB Sen 94.1, Ppr 96.0, F1 95.0; SVEB Sen 64.8, Ppr 89.0, F1 75.0; overall 97.2 — [PMC10933543](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933543/)
- Alforja & Aban, medRxiv 2026 [preprint] with code: staged ablation on DS1/DS2 (model selection on 5 record-grouped DS1 folds; DS2 not used for selection): S1 beat waveform only (255 inputs) -> S2 + 6 RR features -> S3 + 7 "rhythm history and waveform context" features -> S4 + 8 waveform descriptors. "Adding the six RR features raised DS2 macro F1 by 0.025 to 0.093 across model families" (logistic regression, random forest, XGBoost, 1D CNN) with bootstrap intervals excluding zero; final macro F1 XGBoost 0.580, CNN 0.574; random forest at S2 had 94.45 % accuracy but detected 26 of 1,837 S beats (as reported in the README) — [GitHub README](https://github.com/janusaban/interpatient-ecg-classification); preprint DOI as listed there: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.04.26364683
- JTEHM 2022 (IEEE J Transl Eng Health Med), random forest + "segment label" (a CNN trained on PhysioNet/CinC 2017 labels the ECG segment containing the beat; "the label will be assigned to each heartbeat in this segment"): SVEB F1 0.77, Ppr 0.76, Sen 0.78 (RFS_b) vs its stated basis Saenz-Cogollo et al. (6 features) SVEB F1 0.73, Ppr 0.68, Sen 0.79; both Acc 0.96. Authors: "the segment label information could help ... for the heartbeat labels which requires the context information of the ECG". This is a comparison with prior work, not a same-pipeline ablation — [PMC9455809](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9455809/)
- Lee S. et al., CMPB 269:108898, 2025: varies the number of beats per input for a 1-D CNN; "peak accuracy at four beats under inter-patient conditions, with minimal improvements beyond this point" (94.82 % accuracy). Split is fivefold patient-wise CV on HiCardi wearable data + MIT-BIH with 8 multi-label arrhythmias, NOT DS1/DS2 (abstract only) — [PubMed 40554829](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40554829/)
- Niu et al., JBHI 2020: "The ablation study on our method validates the effectiveness of the proposed symbolization approach and joint representation architecture" (symbolization jointly represents "the morphology and rhythm of the heartbeat"); numbers not seen (abstract only) — [PubMed 31545750](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31545750/)
- Order tests: in the 190-record PubMed sample, no abstract mentions shuffling / permuting / order of beats; none of the full texts read here (Mousavi, Guo, Self-ONN, Zhang 2021, Wu 2022, iScience 2024, JTEHM 2022, PeerJ 2020, Wang 2021) contains a beat-order or RR-order shuffle test — [PubMed search](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=%28inter-patient%5Btiab%5D+OR+interpatient%5Btiab%5D%29+AND+%28ECG%5Btiab%5D+OR+heartbeat%5Btiab%5D%29)
- Qualitative evidence that order / longer context matters for S: the Self-ONN paper notes "In some records (e.g., patients 202, 222, 232 and 234) several S beats are present in the sequence, yet only the first S beat displays the timing anomaly ... Patient 234 has an episode of junctional tachycardia ... 50 consecutive beats of supraventricular ectopy", and "All algorithms that target beat-by-beat classification will eventually suffer" on such records — [arXiv 2204.03768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03768)

### Inferences
- S-class gain from RR context, per ablation: +72.5 pp Se and +48.6 pp precision (Zhang 2021; whole-record + 10-beat past normalisation); +63.9 pp Se and +34.8 pp +P (Wu 2022; window widened to about ±0.7 s around the R peak, both directions); +4.1 pp Se, +7.7 pp Ppr (iScience EXP2, patient-adapted split); +1.8 pp Se, +6.6 pp Ppr (iScience EXP3, non-standard split); +0.025..0.093 macro F1 (medRxiv). V-class gains are much smaller (Zhang 2021: +3.8 pp Se; iScience: -0.1 to -1.9 pp Se, +1.6 to +3.4 pp Ppr). The size of the gain depends on how weak the single-beat baseline is: when the beat-only model almost never predicts S (Se 6.3 %, 16.3 %), RR features rescue it; on stronger baselines the gain is a few points.
- The Self-ONN observation (runs of S beats where only the first is premature) is a concrete case where pre-RR / local RR is not enough and a longer, ordered past (rhythm onset) would be needed; nobody has quantified this with an ablation.

### Gaps
- Sellami & Hwang 2019 single-beat vs multi-beat ablation: full text not accessible; their patent says only that adding the neighbouring beat "can improve" accuracy, with no number.
- Niu 2020 ablation numbers, Lin & Yang 2014 normalized-vs-raw RR comparison, Mondéjar-Guerra 2019 per-feature-set results (RR alone vs morphology), de Chazal 2004 12-configuration table: full texts not reachable (IEEE / Wiley / ScienceDirect returned 403 or JS pages).
- No paper found that tests causal (past-only) vs bidirectional context, or that shuffles beat / RR order.

## Q3. Verification of the named examples and other 2018-2026 papers (per-paper catalogue)

### Takeaway
All ten named papers exist as described, but several details differ from the usual summary: Guo 2019 classifies 10-s segments (majority label), not beats; Mousavi 2019's public code built test "sequences" from class-sorted beats; Sellami 2019's "multi-beat" input is the target beat plus the preceding beat (per the patent); most RR-feature papers use post-RR and/or record-wide averages (future information).

### Inferences
- Under DS1->DS2, S-class results cluster around Se 60-83 % with +P 30-91 %; the outliers above 88 % Se with >90 % PPV (Mousavi 2019; capsule + Seq2Seq 2022) are the sequence-to-sequence models, which is exactly where the sequence-construction problem in Q4 applies. Whether the 2022 capsule paper and Jiang 2020 inherit the Mousavi code's sequence construction was not checked.
- For a paper claiming a causal multi-beat model, the closest prior work is Sellami & Hwang (target + previous beat, per patent) and the CBM 2026 past-only features; neither tests order.

- Per-class tables of Niu 2020, Mondéjar-Guerra 2019, Sellami 2019, Takalo-Mattila 2018, Lin & Yang 2014 were seen only in secondary sources; secondary sources disagree in places (e.g. Wu 2022 credits Sellami with accuracy 89.91 % while Self-ONN Table IV gives Sellami 88.34 % and Takalo 89.91 %).

## Q4. Validity concerns raised about these methods (leakage, test-patient data, future beats)

### Takeaway
Yes, several. The most serious is for sequence-to-sequence models: the public Mousavi & Afghah code formed train and test sequences from beats sorted by class, so each sequence carried one label, which the author acknowledged on GitHub. Others: post-RR and record-wide RR averages use future beats and statistics of the whole test record; some "inter-patient" results add test-patient beats to training or classify segments rather than beats; DS2 is often used for model selection; the S class is dominated by a few DS2 records.

### Cited Findings
- Mousavi & Afghah seq2seq code, GitHub issue #2 (2019-08-23): "the heartbeats of each kind (one of N S V) are selected and shuffled ... concatenated by rows ... split into a sequence every max_time (the default is 10) line ... the labels of each sequence are almost the same, and the heartbeats contained in each sequence are not split from the same record at all"; another user (2019-12-31): "both the training and testing sequences contain one label type only"; author reply (2019-09-02): "Your point is correct ... the sequences should be composed of heartbeats from the same ECG record. Unfortunately, I had not updated the code on the Github"; later (2019-12-31): "Yes, it is possible. It depends on the sequence length"; the reporter (2020-10-15): "this means knowing the label of the test heartbeat in advance, which is unrealistic" [GitHub, informal] — [issue #2](https://github.com/MousaviSajad/ECG-Heartbeat-Classification-seq2seq-model/issues/2)
- The paper itself describes the opposite procedure ("Splitting the continuous ECG signal to a sequence of heartbeats") and teacher forcing in training only ("the input (the shifted target) is just used during the training phase") — [arXiv 1812.07421](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07421)
- Model selection on the test set is common practice in this line: a 2025 GitHub discussion under the same repository states "在患者间范式下，DS1为训练集，DS2为测试集，没有验证集" (in the inter-patient paradigm DS1 is training, DS2 is test, there is no validation set) [GitHub, informal] — [issue #14](https://github.com/MousaviSajad/ECG-Heartbeat-Classification-seq2seq-model/issues/14); by contrast the 2026 medRxiv study selects models on "five record-grouped DS1 folds" and states DS2 labels were not used for fitting or selection [preprint] — [GitHub README](https://github.com/janusaban/interpatient-ecg-classification)
- Future-beat dependence of post-RR: a 2020 paper (search snippet, page 403) notes post-RR needs the next beat, so classification is delayed until "the following beat is detected", and that record-wise normalisation uses many future beats, which "may not suit ambulatory or real-time use" — [doi 10.3390/a13040075](https://doi.org/10.3390/a13040075)
- Record-wide (test-record) statistics in RR features: Zhang J. 2021 pre-RR ratio uses "the average of all pre-RR intervals of the corresponding recording" — [PMC8181174](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8181174/); Wang T. 2021 subtracts "the average RR-interval" — [PMC7831114](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7831114/); Mondéjar-Guerra code divides each RR feature by the patient average — [GitHub](https://github.com/mondejar/ecg-classification); Self-ONN averages RR "over ±10 seconds from the current beat" — [arXiv 2204.03768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03768)
- Test-patient beats in training: iScience 2024 EXP2 adds "the first 5 min of each record from the test set ... to the training set for auxiliary training" (this is the patient-adaptive protocol, not pure inter-patient) — [PMC10933543](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933543/); Guo 2019 excludes from its comparison works "that have used a good number of annotated beats from the testing records for training purposes" — [arXiv 1810.04121](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04121)
- Segment labels instead of beat labels: Guo 2019 labels each 10-s segment by its most frequent non-N annotation and slides one beat at a time, giving 4,225 SVEB test samples from DS2 records (DS2 has about 1,836 S beats per Mousavi's count), so its SVEB metrics are not beat-level — [arXiv 1810.04121](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04121), [arXiv 1812.07421](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07421)
- SMOTE leakage concern raised by Mousavi & Afghah against earlier work: "The augmented data ... should not be considered in the validation phase ... although the work reported in [18] followed this approach, we believe that the results are not quite accurate" — [arXiv 1812.07421](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07421); MKP-ECG 2026 reports "methodologically corrected data augmentation (waveform-only SMOTE with nearest-neighbor clinical feature copying ...)" — [PubMed 43077017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077017/)
- S-class concentration: "Record 232 holds 75% of DS2 supraventricular beats" [preprint] — [GitHub README](https://github.com/janusaban/interpatient-ecg-classification); an EMBC 2019 paper "aims to question the current protocol followed to report heartbeat classification results, which impedes the improvement of the SVEB class without falling on over-fitting" and analyses the MIT-BIH composition — [PubMed 31947298](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31947298/)
- Leakage-controlled framing in 2026: "reported performance often relies on intra-patient evaluation ... We investigate this question under a leakage-controlled inter-patient protocol. ... descriptors computed only from preceding beats ... to prevent leakage of patient identity or future information" — [PubMed 42727300](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42727300/)
- Inconsistent numbers between versions of the same paper: Self-ONN arXiv v2 (S Sen 83.37 / Ppr 83.51) vs TBME abstract (S recall 82.50 / precision 82.19) — [arXiv 2204.03768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03768), [PubMed 35786545](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35786545/); Guo abstract (SVEB F1 61.25) vs table (61.94) — [arXiv 1810.04121](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04121); Sellami patent table vs text (PPV 48.25 vs 55.59 %) — [US11298067B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11298067)

### Inferences
- Any sequence-to-sequence inter-patient result that copies or builds on the Mousavi code (Jiang 2020 BIBM explicitly extends that model; the 2022 capsule + Seq2Seq paper reports S SEN 99.56 %) should be treated as unverified until its sequences are shown to be consecutive beats from one record. Their near-perfect S numbers are not evidence that beat context works.
- For a causal "previous beats only" claim, the fair baselines must not use post-RR, ±10 s averages, record-wide RR averages, bidirectional RNNs, or centred segments; most published RR-feature results use at least one of these, so they are not directly comparable to a causal model.
- The largest published S-class context gains (Zhang 2021, Wu 2022) come from Hindawi's Journal of Healthcare Engineering and use partly non-causal features; they show the direction but should not be cited as precise effect sizes.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper was found that formally re-evaluates the Mousavi seq2seq model with record-consecutive sequences; the evidence is the GitHub thread only.
- I did not check whether Jiang 2020 or the 2022 capsule paper reused the class-sorted sequence construction.
- No paper found that reports a shuffled-order control (beats or RR values) under DS1/DS2.
