# Spiking cross-attention, two-stream fusion, and softmax-retaining attention in SNNs (candidates for coupling Neocortex and Hippocampus)

"Venue verified" means checked on an official proceedings page (CVF Open Access, NeurIPS proceedings, PMLR/mlanthology) or via an indexed DOI (OpenAlex / Semantic Scholar). Context recap for the report writer (from the assignment, not from sources): the target model computes binary q (Neocortex, one time step copied to 4) and binary k, v (Hippocampus, T_h = 4), scores = q k^T * D^-0.5 + learned relative-position bias, no softmax, out = LIF(scores @ v) -> Linear+BN+LIF = y, then gates Hippocampus spikes x <- x * (1 - y). D = 64, N = 24 / 84 / 180.

## Q1. Spiking multimodal / two-stream transformers with cross-attention (top venues 2022-2026), and the cross-attention form they use

### Takeaway
True spike-only cross-attention between two sources exists but is rare at top venues, and nearly all of it reuses the softmax-free Spikformer form SN(Q K^T V * s) with Q from one stream and K/V from the other (or joint attention over concatenated tokens). Ablations against simpler fusion are small and mostly from classification/tracking: about +0.5 to +3 points over add/sum/direct fusion, often confounded with other changes; no paper found ablates spiking cross-attention against concat/add/gating in a regression or forecasting task. Many "spiking multimodal transformers" at top venues are hybrids where the cross-attention itself is real-valued (ANN) with softmax.

### Cited Findings

**A. Spike-domain cross-attention (Q from one source, K/V from another)**

- S-CMRL (He, Zhao, Dong, Shen, Yang, Zeng; audio-visual). Venue: **[arXiv-only, lower reliability]**; "manuscript is under review"; Semantic Scholar lists venue arXiv.org only. Code: https://github.com/Brain-Cog-Lab/S-CMRL — [arXiv 2502.12488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12488)
  - Exact computation: base attention is Spikformer SSA (their Eq. 7): Q = SN_Q(BN(xW_Q)), K = SN_K(BN(xW_K)), V = SN_V(BN(xW_V)), SSA(x) = SN(BN(Linear(SN(Q K^T V * s)))), s = scaling factor, no softmax. For cross-modal use, "the input to the query Q_s is the audio modality feature x^a and the inputs to the key K_s and value V_s are the visual modality features x^v" — [arXiv 2502.12488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12488)
  - Fusion is residual: g_a(x^a) = x^a + alpha * CCSSA_a(x^a, x^v) (Eq. 10-11); best alpha = 1.5; performance degraded when alpha was raised further ("validates the necessity of retaining the original features") — [arXiv 2502.12488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12488)
  - The cross-attention output is not used token-by-token: spatial cross-attention output S^a is averaged over the N tokens (Eq. 12) and broadcast back (Eq. 13); a temporal cross-attention (attention along the T axis, inputs rearranged to B x N x T x D) is averaged over T (Eq. 14) and broadcast back (Eq. 15); the two are multiplied element-wise (Eq. 16). So the cross-modal term is a per-(t, channel) vector times a per-(token, channel) vector — [arXiv 2502.12488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12488)
  - Spike-only vs real-valued: Q, K, V binary; Q K^T V * s is integer-valued then passed through SN; the averaged S_reduced / T_reduced and the residual sum are real-valued — [arXiv 2502.12488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12488)
  - Differing T / lengths: not handled; both modalities use T = 4, static images are replicated over T, spectrograms are resized to the image size (128 x 128) "to ensure uniform multimodal input" — [arXiv 2502.12488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12488)
  - Gains vs simpler fusion (Table II, "w/o CCSSA" = features fed directly to the network for audio-visual integration, i.e., direct fusion): CREMA-D 69.62% -> 72.72% (spatial-only 70.70%, temporal-only 70.83%); UrbanSound8K-AV 97.44% -> 98.05% (spatial-only 97.82%, temporal-only 97.79%). Adding the semantic alignment loss (Table III): 72.72% -> 73.25% (CREMA-D), 98.05% -> 98.13% (UrbanSound8K-AV) — [arXiv 2502.12488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12488)
  - Against other spiking fusion methods re-implemented by the authors (text describing Table I; the table itself was an image): CREMA-D S-CMRL 73.25% vs WeightAttention 64.78%, SCA 66.53%, CMCI 70.02%; UrbanSound8K-AV 98.13% vs CMCI 97.90%, WeightAttention 97.60%, SCA 97.44% — [arXiv 2502.12488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12488)
  - Conflict: a later paper re-ran S-CMRL and reports CREMA-D 71.59% / UrbanSound8K-AV 98.05% (its Table 1), not 73.25% / 98.13% — [arXiv 2602.00701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00701)

- SNNergy / CMQKA "Cross-Modal Binary Attention" (Saleh, Ahmadi; audio-visual). Venue: **[arXiv-only, lower reliability]** (posted 2026-01-31) — [arXiv 2602.00701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00701)
  - Exact computation: Q_v from video spikes, K_a from audio spikes, both {0,1}^{T x C x N}; binary token mask A_t = SN(sum_c Q_v[t, c, :]) in {0,1}^{T x 1 x N} (Eq. 7); complementary feature S_{v<-a} = A_t ⊙ K_a (element-wise mask on the other modality's key); learnable residual fusion. O(N) complexity; there is no pairwise q-k score at all (a QKFormer-style token mask). Stage 3 falls back to quadratic SSA — [arXiv 2602.00701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00701)
  - Differing T / lengths: requires the same T and N in both modalities (element-wise mask); all inputs 128 x 128 and T = 6 — [arXiv 2602.00701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00701)
  - Results (Table 1): CREMA-D 78.38%, AVE 72.14%, UrbanSound8K-AV 99.66%. Within-paper ablation only of spatial vs temporal parts (Table 3): AVE spatial-only 63.68 / temporal-only 70.15 / spatiotemporal 72.14; CREMA-D 77.78 / 77.24 / 78.38. No "replace CMQKA by concat/add" ablation — [arXiv 2602.00701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00701)
  - Simple-fusion reference points in the same Table 1: TAAF-SNNs (reported numbers) concat 77.55 vs sum 76.75 (CREMA-D), 70.65 vs 68.91 (AVE); ANN OGM-EG sum 74.80 / concat 74.02 / FiLM 76.46 / gated 72.67 on CREMA-D — [arXiv 2602.00701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00701)

- SMMT, "Transformer-Based Spiking Neural Networks for Multimodal Audiovisual Classification" (Guo et al.). Venue verified via DOI listing: IEEE Transactions on Cognitive and Developmental Systems vol. 16 no. 3, pp. 1077-1086 (online 24 Oct 2023), DOI 10.1109/TCDS.2023.3327081. Uses unimodal spiking subnetworks plus a "spiking cross-attention module for fusion"; datasets released at GitHub Guo-Lingyue/SMMT. **Abstract only; exact computation and ablation numbers not read** — [XJTU scholar listing](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/en/publications/transformer-based-spiking-neural-networks-for-multimodal-audiovis/); [Unpaywall DOI record](https://unpaywall.org/10.1109%2FTCDS.2023.3327081)

- SNNSIR Spike Stereo Cross-Attention (SSCA) between left and right stereo views (two streams with identical shapes). **[arXiv-only, lower reliability]**; "code will be available after the article is accepted"; the SSCA equations could not be extracted from the PDF text — [arXiv 2508.12271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12271)

**B. Two streams fused by joint spiking self-attention over concatenated tokens (template-search / frame-event)**

- SpikeFET (Yang, Fan, Zhang, Lian, Shen, Hu; frame + event single-object tracking). Venue: author-stated "Accepted by NeurIPS2025" (arXiv comment; not checked on the NeurIPS proceedings site). Code: https://github.com/Noctis-A/SpikeFET — [arXiv 2505.20834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20834)
  - Exact computation: image and event feature maps get positional, modality and type encodings, are concatenated U = [U_i; U_e] and passed through Transformer Spike Blocks with Cross-Shaped-Window SSA: Q_S = SN(Linear(U)), K_S = SN(Linear(U)), V_S = SN(Linear_gamma(U)); SSA(Q_S, K_S, V_S) = SN(Q_S K_S^T V_S * scale) (Eq. 9-11). "To address the challenge of large values generated by matrix multiplication, a scaling factor (scale) is introduced to regulate the magnitude." Template frames and the search frame are also concatenated spatially (Random Patchwork Module) — [arXiv 2505.20834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20834)
  - Gains vs simpler fusion (Table 4, SpikeFET-Tiny; FE108 AUC / PR, VisEvent AUC / PR): default (token concat + joint attention) 68.5 / 96.5, 56.8 / 73.5; "Concat -> Add" 68.0 / 95.4, 55.2 / 71.9; "Pre-Concat" (image-level early fusion) 67.5 / 95.9, 55.6 / 72.3; w/o modality encodings 68.0 / 96.1, 55.8 / 72.6; frame only 51.8 / 76.2, 54.6 / 71.1; event only 64.4 / 94.7, 41.0 / 57.1 — [arXiv 2505.20834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20834)

- SDTrack (event-based tracking). Template and search event frames are concatenated before the backbone ("IPL"), then spike-driven attention, then template-search cross-correlation. Venue: **[arXiv-only per available evidence]** (v4, June 2026; venue not verified). Code: https://github.com/YmShan/SDTrack — [arXiv 2503.08703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08703)

**C. Hybrid models (spiking front-end, real-valued softmax cross-attention) — the cross-attention is not spike-domain**

- Spiking Tucker Fusion Transformer (audio-visual zero-shot). Author-stated "Accepted by TIP" (IEEE TIP 2024). Joint Reasoning Module: Q_av = MHCA(Y^a, Y^v) (Eq. 14), standard multi-head cross-attention on real-valued features, weights shared across modalities. Table VI (UCF-GZSL): Transformer+MLP HM 23.28, Spikformer+SNN HM 25.72, Transformer+SNN (STFT) HM 32.58 — [arXiv 2407.08130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08130)
- MDST++ (audio-visual zero-shot). Author-stated "Accepted by IEEE TCSVT". Recurrent joint-knowledge unit: C^a_t = CA(AP(a_t), AP(h_t)), C^v_t = CA(AP(v_t), AP(h_t)); h_t = C^av_t * h_{t-1} + (1 - C^av_t) * S^ahv_t (a gated recurrent memory updated through cross-attention); audio pathway R^a = CA(a_t, a_t * Sigmoid(j^a_t)) where j^a_t is the SNN output (SNN used as a sigmoid gate); final Z^av = MHCA(P^v, P^a). The SNN is split into m stages with "progressively compressed time steps (T_1 > T_2 > ... > T_m)" — [arXiv 2505.19938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19938)
- HDI-Former (frames + events detection; hybrid ANN-SNN). In the SNN branch the attention map keeps softmax on spiking Q/K plus a relative-position term: W^l_e = SoftMax(Q_e K_e^T / sqrt(d_e) + lambda_1 P + lambda_2 S_e). Venue: **[arXiv-only, lower reliability]** (venue not verified) — [arXiv 2411.18658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18658)
- ISTASTrack (RGB-event tracking, ANN + SNN branches with an ISTA adapter). Author-stated IEEE TIP, DOI 10.1109/TIP.2026.3694138. In its SNN branch "attention weights are computed through dot-product operations omitting the softmax function due to the non-negativity of spikes" — [arXiv 2509.09977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09977)
- SpikeVLA (vision-language-action). arXiv journal-ref "ICML 2026". Built by conversion; LayerNorm, GELU and Softmax in attention are realized by a conversion mechanism, i.e., not a directly trained spiking cross-attention — [arXiv 2606.27807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27807)
- SpikeCLIP (spiking CLIP-style). Semantic Scholar lists it in Neural Networks vol. 188; it is a dual-encoder contrastive model (image and text encoders aligned by cosine similarity / KL distillation); no cross-attention between streams found in the figure text — [arXiv 2310.06488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06488); [Semantic Scholar record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2310.06488)

**D. Spiking video (time steps as tokens)**

- SpikeVideoFormer (ICML 2025; venue per Semantic Scholar and arXiv comment). Joint space-time spike-driven attention over all T x N tokens with Hamming-similarity scores (details in Q4). Ablation (Table 6): Joint -> Factorized space/time attention PA-MPJPE 39.8 -> 45.1 (worse), mIoU 73.1 -> 70.3; Joint -> Spatial-only 54.2 / 62.1. Code: https://github.com/JimmyZou/SpikeVideoFormer — [arXiv 2505.10352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10352)

### Inferences
- Cross-attention gains over add/direct fusion in spiking models are of the order of 0.5-3 points on classification/tracking (SpikeFET concat vs add: -0.5 AUC FE108, -1.6 AUC VisEvent; S-CMRL CCSSA vs direct: +3.10 / +0.61). These are single-run numbers without seeds or error bars, so they are weak evidence; a "no clear difference" on ETT is not out of line with this literature.
- The most-cited spiking cross-attention form (S-CMRL, SpikeFET, SMMT lineage) is exactly the form already in the NSMT coupling (binary Q from one stream, binary K/V from the other, Q K^T V scaled, LIF, no softmax). The published versions add (a) a residual with a tuned strength alpha rather than a multiplicative inhibitory gate, (b) modality/type encodings, and (c) for S-CMRL, pooling the cross-attention output to a low-rank modulation. None of these papers use an inhibitory x*(1-y) gate.
- S-CMRL's temporal pooling (average over T, then broadcast) and MDST++'s compressed-time stages are the only explicit treatments of time-axis mismatch found; nearly every other paper forces both streams to share T and N. For NSMT, where the Neocortex has one step and the Hippocampus has 4, copying the Neocortex output to 4 steps is consistent with what the literature does (S-CMRL replicates static images over T).

### Gaps
- No spiking cross-attention paper for time-series regression/forecasting was found; all evidence is from classification, tracking, detection, zero-shot.
- SMMT full text (exact cross-attention equations, ablation vs concat) was not read; S-CMRL's Table I and CMA's tables are images and only the numbers quoted in the text could be extracted.
- SpikeFET's NeurIPS 2025 acceptance and SDTrack's venue were not verified on official proceedings (DBLP/OpenReview blocked; OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar rate-limited during this session).
- Spiking RGB-D cross-attention and spiking audio-visual models at CVPR/ICCV/ECCV with spike-domain cross-attention were not found in this search; this may be a real absence or a search limitation.

## Q2. Spiking memory / key-value retrieval attention and spiking gating-fusion alternatives

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed spiking transformer that retrieves from an explicit key-value memory (Hopfield-style) through spiking cross-attention was found. The closest published alternatives to cross-attention are cross-stream gates computed from firing rates (CMA, Neural Networks 2024), binary token masks from one stream applied to the other (CMQKA, arXiv), residual fusion with a strength hyperparameter (S-CMRL), and sigmoid gates / gated recurrent memories in hybrids (MDST++).

### Cited Findings
- CMA, "Enhancing SNN-based spatio-temporal learning: a benchmark dataset and Cross-Modality Attention model" (Zhou et al.). Venue verified via DOI: Neural Networks 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.neunet.2024.106677 — [OpenAlex-indexed DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2024.106677); [arXiv 2410.15689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15689)
  - Exact computation: event stream gives a temporal firing-rate vector r_e^n (mean spikes per time step over C x H x W, Eq. 7); frame stream gives a spatial firing-rate map r_f^n (mean over T x C, Eq. 8). Temporal scores d_e = sigma(M_2 delta(M_1 r_e)) (two FC layers, Eq. 9); spatial scores d_f = sigma(M_3 * r_f) (3 x 3 conv, Eq. 10). Cross-fusion: frame features are scaled per time step by the event-derived temporal score (Eq. 11), event features are scaled per location by the frame-derived spatial score (Eq. 12); outputs concatenated. The authors note that, unlike Attention SNN (Yao et al. 2023) "which uses the membrane potential as the input", they use firing rates — [arXiv 2410.15689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15689)
  - Reported: CMA improves accuracy for LIF, IF, PLIF and LIAF neurons over "simple middle feature concat fusion" (Table 10), with a smaller gain for LIAF (analog output). Exact numbers are in image tables and could not be extracted — [arXiv 2410.15689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15689)
  - Differing T: event and frame streams share T in this model (T = 4 / 10 / 20 tested) — [arXiv 2410.15689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15689)
- Attention Spiking Neural Networks (Yao et al.), IEEE TPAMI 2023, DOI 10.1109/tpami.2023.3241201: attention module that takes the membrane potential as input (per the CMA paper's description). Not read in full here — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpami.2023.3241201); description in [arXiv 2410.15689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15689)
- CMQKA binary token mask (A_t = SN(sum_c Q_v), S = A_t ⊙ K_a) is effectively a spike-domain gate from one stream onto the other; see Q1 — [arXiv 2602.00701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00701)
- MDST++ gated recurrent joint knowledge h_t = C_t * h_{t-1} + (1 - C_t) * S_t, with C_t from cross-attention to the memory h_t (real-valued, hybrid) — [arXiv 2505.19938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19938)
- IEMF (inverse-effectiveness-driven multimodal fusion, applied to ANN and SNN): fusion weighting driven by unimodal strength; Semantic Scholar lists Science Advances vol. 12, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.ady8751. Code: https://github.com/Brain-Cog-Lab/IEMF. Mechanism details and numbers not extracted — [arXiv 2505.10176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10176); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ady8751)
- Web search found Hopfield-attention work for ANNs (e.g., Hopfield-layer variants) and a 2026 arXiv memory-augmented spiking network for neuromorphic vision (arXiv 2603.08730) that combines spiking layers with associative memory; the search summary reports that its mechanisms "trade off against each other". Not read; **[arXiv-only, unverified]** — [arXiv 2603.08730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.08730)

### Inferences
- The NSMT gate x <- x*(1-y) is an inhibitory, multiplicative spike gate driven by a cross-attention output. The closest published analogues gate with a value derived from the other stream's firing rate (CMA) or a binary per-token mask (CMQKA); both require the gating signal to be non-trivially active. If y is mostly silent, an inhibitory gate degenerates to identity, which would make "with Neocortex" and "without Neocortex" behave alike. This is a hypothesis to measure (firing rate of y), not a finding.
- CMA's split ("when" from the stream with temporal resolution, "where" from the stream with spatial detail) maps naturally onto NSMT: the Neocortex runs along the token axis and could provide a per-token (or per-token, per-channel) gain for the Hippocampus, which avoids pairwise q-k scores altogether.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed spiking key-value memory / Hopfield retrieval attention used as cross-attention was found; 2603.08730 was not read.
- No paper directly compared membrane-potential fusion vs spike fusion between two spiking streams with numbers that could be extracted.

## Q3. ANN-to-SNN conversion and spiking softmax approximations that keep normalised attention; cost in time steps and energy

### Takeaway
Exact or near-exact softmax in SNNs is achieved either by computing a real softmax on time-accumulated inputs every step (SpikeZIP-TF: not spike-only, extra O(n^2) memory, T = 64-128), by one-spike phase coding plus a winner-take-all spike shift and threshold scaling (SpikedAttention: spike-only, T = 40, needs a calibrated max and dedicated neuron hardware), or by power-of-two / bit-shift approximations (Sorbet PTsoftmax, BSA Shiftmax, plug-and-play LIF-population operators). Direct-training work shows that softmax only helps if the q-k scores are signed / bell-shaped; with binary q and k, adding softmax gave no gain.

### Cited Findings
- SpikeZIP-TF (You et al.), ICML 2024 (PMLR vol. 235, pp. 57367-57383) — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2024/you2024icml-spikeziptf); [arXiv 2406.03470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03470)
  - Spike-Softmax / Spike-LayerNorm (Eq. 5): X_T = sum_{t=0..T} X_{s,t}; O_T = sigma(X_T); O_{s,t} = O_t - O_{t-1}, where sigma is softmax or LayerNorm; "equivalent to Softmax (LayerNorm) by summing up O_{s,t} through time" — i.e., the operator stores the accumulated real-valued input and evaluates a real softmax every step — [arXiv 2406.03470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03470)
  - Cost (Table 3): Spike-Softmax spatial O(n^2), temporal O(T n^2); "Spike-Softmax and Spike-LayerNorm have n^2 and nd times more complexity than ANN ... requires extra memory to store the accumulated input" — [arXiv 2406.03470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03470)
  - Results: ImageNet 83.82% with SViT-L-32Level, 304.33M params, T = 64 (Table 5); SST-2 93.79% with the 355M Roberta-L source, T = 128 (Table 6) — [arXiv 2406.03470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03470)
- SpikedAttention (Hwang, Lee, Park, Lee, Kung), NeurIPS 2024 (official proceedings). Code: github.com/sangwoohwang/SpikedAttention — [NeurIPS 2024 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/7c9341ad0263428b5057d92f4d88dfa0-Paper-Conference.pdf)
  - One-spike phase coding (each neuron fires once; spike at time t carries B^-t). Trace-driven Q K^T: the earlier-spiking neuron stores its trace B^-t; when the partner spikes, the stored trace is added weighted by the current phase, bounding multiplications by T — [NeurIPS 2024 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/7c9341ad0263428b5057d92f4d88dfa0-Paper-Conference.pdf)
  - WOSS softmax: approximates exp(z_i)/exp(max z) by a spike time t_i with log_B(...) = (z'_i - max z') M_z / ln B ≈ -t_i (Eq. 4); M_z (max of z) is "extracted by running a proxy training dataset"; the first (winner) spike triggers global inhibition and all spikes are shifted so the winner fires at t = 0; the row sum is applied by amplifying the threshold of the next trace-driven dot product: V_theta(t+1) = B * V_theta(t) + sum_j S_WOSS(i,j)(t) (Eq. 8). Final output approximates softmax(S_Q S_K^T / sqrt(d)) S_V — [NeurIPS 2024 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/7c9341ad0263428b5057d92f4d88dfa0-Paper-Conference.pdf)
  - Cost: ImageNet Swin-T conversion with T = 40, B = 1.15: 80.0% with 28.7M params; 42% lower energy than the ANN (45.4 mJ) with 0.6% accuracy loss; GLUE (MA-BERT) 0.3% average loss, 58% less energy; WOSS neurons add 9.88% area and 12.35% power over LIF-only hardware (Appendix C). GELU -> ReLU and LayerNorm -> BatchNorm were required in the source model — [NeurIPS 2024 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/7c9341ad0263428b5057d92f4d88dfa0-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Sorbet (Tang, Yan, Wong), ICML 2025 (PMLR vol. 267, pp. 58989-59004). PTsoftmax replaces softmax with powers of two and approximates the normalising sum by its nearest power of two so division is a bit shift; a stated bound keeps PTsoftmax within a constant factor (1/(2 sqrt 2) to 2 sqrt 2) of softmax; Bit-Shifting PowerNorm replaces LayerNorm. **Secondary summary only; full text not read** — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/tang25l.html); [arXiv 2409.15298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15298)
- Plug-and-Play Spiking Operators (Yuan, Peng, Gu, Xiong). arXiv comment "Accepted to ICML 2026" (not officially verified). Decomposes Softmax, SiLU and normalisation into division, exponentiation and l2-norm primitives realised by LIF neuron groups plus bit-shift scaling; "less than a 1% accuracy drop across all evaluated tasks" without fine-tuning; LLaMA conversions evaluated at T in {1, 2, 4} — [arXiv 2605.20289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20289)
- Training-free conversion with Multi-basis Exponential Decay (MBE) neurons (Wang, Deng, ... Zhang), AAAI 2026 (DOI 10.1609/aaai.v40i3.37195). Softmax is decomposed into exponential, reciprocal and FP multiplication, each approximated by MBE neurons; "less than 1% conversion loss when N >= 4" bases — [arXiv 2508.07710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07710); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i3.37195)
- CSDformer (conversion to a fully spike-driven transformer). **[arXiv-only, lower reliability]**. Replaces softmax by NReLU(x) = ReLU(x / N), N = sequence length, motivated by softmax outputs having expectation 1/N; tailored attention TSA(Q, K, V) = NReLU(f(Q) f(K)^T / sqrt(d_k)) f(V), f(X) = ReLU(BN(X)) (Eq. 6-7); 76.36% ImageNet at 7 time steps. Key observation: "outputs without softmax exhibit excessively large magnitudes, which critically contributes to training divergence (e.g., gradient explosion)" — [arXiv 2509.17461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17461)
- Bipolar Self-attention (BSA) with Shiftmax (Wang, Zhang, et al.), NeurIPS 2025 (official proceedings) — [NeurIPS 2025 abstract](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/9316cfcb0a81e53a1f35b4353f115571-Abstract-Conference.html)
  - Shiftmax(x)_i = 2^{x_i - gamma(x)}, gamma(x) = ceil(log2 sum_i 2^{x_i}) (Eq. 13): a power-of-two numerator with the normaliser rounded up to a power of two, so the row sum lies in (1/2, 1] ("partly maintains row-stochasticity") and "can directly shift the membrane potential of V" (Fig. 3). (Bound reconstructed from the extracted Eq. 14; verify against the PDF.)
  - Ablation on CIFAR-100, Spikingformer (Table 4): binary Q K^T (BMP) no normalisation 79.13%, BMP + Softmax 79.07%, BMP + Shiftmax 79.14%; ternary Q K^T (TMP) no normalisation 79.27%, TMP + Softmax 80.76%, TMP + Shiftmax 80.48%. ### Inferences
- For a directly trained, small (D = 64) model with T_h = 4, conversion-style exact softmax (SpikeZIP-TF T = 64-128; SpikedAttention T = 40) is far outside the time-step budget. The practical options are Shiftmax / PTsoftmax-style bit-shift normalisation or CSDformer-style 1/N scaling of ReLU scores.
- BSA's ablation is the most direct evidence for the NSMT question "would softmax help?": with binary q and k (the NSMT case), softmax or Shiftmax added nothing (79.13 vs 79.07 / 79.14); the gain (+1.49 / +1.21) appeared only after making q, k ternary so that scores are zero-mean. This predicts that adding softmax alone to the current coupling is unlikely to help; making q (and/or k) signed first is the prerequisite. This is one CIFAR-100 result on a vision backbone, so it is suggestive, not conclusive for ETT.

### Gaps
- STA (Spatio-Temporal Approximation, reported as ICLR 2024) was not located/read; its softmax operator and cost are not recorded here.
- Sorbet's exact PTsoftmax equations and energy numbers were not read in the full text.
- None of the conversion papers treat cross-attention between two streams with different time steps; all are self-attention conversions.

## Q4. Why softmax-free spiking attention fails or underperforms, and validated remedies

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed analyses identify four failure modes of binary, softmax-free q k^T attention: (1) wrong score scale (positive mean d*p^2 and variance tied to firing rates, so a fixed 1/sqrt(d) is mis-scaled; can prevent convergence), (2) dot product ignores 0-0 agreements and is dominated by spike-count (magnitude) differences, (3) loss of sign/polarity and of row normalisation, and (4) information loss / bimodal score distributions from binarising q, k, v. Validated remedies: firing-rate-aware scale or affine (k*score + b) folded into the threshold, Hamming / alpha-XNOR / distribution-based similarity, ternary (signed) q-k plus Shiftmax, mixed-precision Q/K/V (binary Q, ReLU K, ternary V), and multi-bit (integer) spikes.

### Cited Findings
- Scale (SpikingResformer, Shi, Hao, Yu; CVPR 2024, DOI 10.1109/cvpr52733.2024.00536) — [arXiv 2403.14302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14302)
  - Argument: with spike inputs one "cannot assume that they possess a mean of 0 and a variance of 1", so the vanilla 1/sqrt(d) factor is not appropriate; "the surrogate gradient also suffers gradient vanishes without scaling". Proposed scales from Theorem 1: c_1 = 1/sqrt(f_X d), c_2 = 1/sqrt(f_Attn HW/p^2), with f the measured average firing rate (radical sign lost in text extraction; verify in PDF) — [arXiv 2403.14302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14302)
  - Ablation (ImageNet-100, Table 3): full model 88.06%; "w/o scaling" does not converge; replacing their scale by 1/sqrt(d) also does not converge ("the scaling factor is the key to convergence"). Supplement Table S1: Spike-driven Transformer-8-384 w/o scaling 83.06% vs with firing-rate scaling 83.96%; SpikingResformer-S with SDSA w/o scaling does not converge, with scaling 87.74% — [arXiv 2403.14302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14302)
- Score mean/variance with binary vs ternary (BSA, NeurIPS 2025, Theorem 1): for q, k ~ N(0, sigma^2) thresholded at theta with p = P(q > theta) = Phi(-theta/sigma) (fraction order garbled in the text extraction; verify in PDF): real product E = 0, Var = d sigma^4; binary product E = d p^2, Var = d p^2 (1 - p^2); ternary product E = 0, Var = 4 d p^2 — "BMP exhibits systematic bias ... from binary activation's exclusion of negative polarity". Also: softmax-free SSA "entirely lacks row-stochasticity constraints. Consequently, attention scores from different query vectors exist on disparate numerical scales" — [NeurIPS 2025 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/9316cfcb0a81e53a1f35b4353f115571-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- 0-0 agreements ignored (alpha-XNOR, Xiao et al.; CVPR 2025, pp. 5444-5454, CVF Open Access) — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Xiao_Rethinking_Spiking_Self-Attention_Mechanism_Implementing_a-XNOR_Similarity_Calculation_in_Spiking_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
  - Claim: "the dot product's ineffectiveness in measuring similarity between spiking queries and keys, due to numerous non-spiking events". alpha-XNOR assigns value alpha to (0, 0) pairs (alpha = 0: dot product; alpha = 1: XNOR). Scores are then mapped by an affine transform Attn' = (k * Score + b) V (Eq. 25), with k and b set from the firing rates f_Q, f_K, f_V via E and Var formulas (Eq. 26-29; gamma_1 = f_Q f_K + alpha (1 - f_Q)(1 - f_K)); "the multiplication ... can be merged into the threshold of this spiking neuron layer" — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Xiao_Rethinking_Spiking_Self-Attention_Mechanism_Implementing_a-XNOR_Similarity_Calculation_in_Spiking_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)
  - Ablation (CIFAR-100, Spikformer, Table 3): baseline 78.21%; + alpha-SSA with alpha = 0 (only the affine score transform) 79.04%; alpha = 1 (XNOR) 79.33%; alpha = 0.3 80.22% — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Xiao_Rethinking_Spiking_Self-Attention_Mechanism_Implementing_a-XNOR_Similarity_Calculation_in_Spiking_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)
- Hamming similarity and threshold scale (SpikeVideoFormer, ICML 2025) — [arXiv 2505.10352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10352)
  - "the dot-product is not well-suited as the attention score ... [it] will indiscriminately disregard the corresponding elements" where the spike query is zero. Score: normalized Hamming similarity f_H(q_s, k_s) = 1 - (1/D) sum_i 1(q_s(i) != k_s(i)), rewritten as 1/2 + (2q_s - 1)^T (2k_s - 1) / (2D) to keep linear complexity; with random-sign embeddings f_H approximates g(cosine) = 1 - arccos(cos)/pi as D grows (Prop., Eq. 5). Attention SN_s with threshold scaled by s — [arXiv 2505.10352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10352)
  - Ablation (Table 6; pose PA-MPJPE lower is better, segmentation mIoU): full model 39.8 / 73.1; Hamming -> dot product 45.7 (+5.9) / 65.9 (-7.8). Threshold scale s = 1/(2D) vs fixed 1/8 (the prior Spike-driven Transformer V2 setting): 46.7 (+6.9) / 68.5 (-4.6); s = 1/64: 43.0 / 71.1; s = 1/512: 41.3 / 70.3 — [arXiv 2505.10352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10352)
- Magnitude mismatch between binary Q and K (SNN-ViT with Saccadic Spike Self-Attention, Wang, Zhang, et al.; ICLR 2025, per the camera-ready header) — [arXiv 2502.12677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12677)
  - "Due to the discrete activation characteristics of spiking neurons ... the magnitude of Q and K in SNNs shows significant variability, which leads to the failure of the Dot-Product in measuring spatial relevance." Remedy: score = negative cross-entropy between firing-rate distributions of q and k (magnitude-insensitive) — [arXiv 2502.12677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12677)
  - Ablation (CIFAR-100, Table 5): Spikformer SSA 76.95%; SSSA with dot product 77.12%; SSSA with LIF instead of saccadic neurons 78.84%; SSSA-V1 79.71%; SSSA-V2 79.60%. "SSSA+DP shows almost no performance improvement compared to the baseline" — [arXiv 2502.12677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12677)
- Information loss from binarising Q/K/V (A2OS2A, Guo, Liu, Chen, Peng, Zhang, Ma; CVPR 2025, CVF Open Access; DOI 10.1109/cvpr52734.2025.02272) — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Guo_Spiking_Transformer_Introducing_Accurate_Addition-Only_Spiking_Self-Attention_for_Transformer_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
  - Q = SN^b(BN(XW_Q)) binary; K = ReLU(BN(XW_K)) real non-negative; V = SN^t(BN(XW_V)) ternary {-1, 0, 1}; A2OS2A = SN(Q K^T V) (Eq. 22-28). Non-negative Q K^T removes the need for softmax "to enforce non-negativity"; the authors also drop the scaling factor because K is unbounded real — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Guo_Spiking_Transformer_Introducing_Accurate_Addition-Only_Spiking_Self-Attention_for_Transformer_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)

ImageNet 78.66% — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Guo_Spiking_Transformer_Introducing_Accurate_Addition-Only_Spiking_Self-Attention_for_Transformer_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)
- Bimodal score distribution / spike information distortion (QSD-Transformer, Qiu, Zhang, et al.; ICLR 2025 per camera-ready header) — [arXiv 2501.13492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13492)
  - "the bimodal distribution of quantized spike-driven self-attention (Q-SDSA) leads to spike information distortion (SID)". Remedy: information-enhanced LIF emitting multi-bit spikes a[t] = (1/b) clip(v[t], 0, b) during training (integer spikes, "inference is spike-driven"), plus fine-grained distillation to align attention distributions with an ANN — [arXiv 2501.13492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13492)

- Scale / magnitude in conversion: CSDformer reports that softmax-free attention outputs have "excessively large magnitudes" causing divergence, fixed by dividing by N (NReLU) — [arXiv 2509.17461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17461)

### Inferences
- The NSMT coupling has the exact configuration these papers diagnose: binary q and k, fixed D^-0.5 scale, no row normalisation, dot-product similarity. With D = 64 and Neocortex firing rate f_q, E[q·k] = 64 f_q f_k; if the Neocortex is sparse (earlier MODI notes said the memoryless population Neocortex "barely fires"), scores are near zero for most queries, LIF(scores @ v) seldom fires, y is mostly 0, and x*(1-y) passes the Hippocampus almost unchanged. That would explain "no clear difference from the model without the Neocortex" on ETT. This is a hypothesis; the direct check is the firing rate of q and of y and the fraction of Hippocampus spikes actually removed by the gate.
- Remedies ranked by evidence quality and fit to a D = 64, T = 4 model (all peer-reviewed): (1) firing-rate-aware scale or affine k*score + b folded into the LIF threshold (SpikingResformer, alpha-XNOR; cheap, no new ops); (2) Hamming / alpha-XNOR similarity so 0-0 agreement counts (SpikeVideoFormer +5.9 / -7.8 when removed; alpha-XNOR +2.01 over baseline on CIFAR-100); (3) signed q/k (ternary) and only then a Shiftmax row normalisation (BSA); (4) real-valued K (A2OS2A) or integer spikes (QSD IE-LIF) to reduce information loss.
- The learned relative-position bias in NSMT adds a real-valued term to an unnormalised integer score; HDI-Former is the only found model that keeps softmax with a relative-position term on spiking Q/K (and it is arXiv-only, hybrid). How a bias interacts with an unnormalised, firing-rate-dependent score scale was not studied in any paper found.

### Gaps
- All failure-mode evidence comes from vision (CIFAR, ImageNet, video, tracking) self-attention, not from cross-attention between streams with different time axes, and not from regression/forecasting.
- No paper found measures attention collapse (all queries attending to the same keys) in spiking attention with numbers; the closest are BSA's row-scale disparity argument and the low-pass analysis (arXiv-only).
- Seeds / variance are not reported in any of the ablations quoted; differences of under about 1 point (e.g., BSA BMP rows) cannot be separated from noise from the papers alone.
