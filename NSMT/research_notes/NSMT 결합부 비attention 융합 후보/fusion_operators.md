# Non-attention fusion operators for two spike streams in SNN prior work (candidates for the Neocortex-Hippocampus coupling)

Date: 2026-11-19. Scope: SNN papers that merge two streams, modalities or pathways with an operator that is **not** query-key cross-attention: spike-wise logic (ADD / AND / IAND / OR / XOR), merging in the membrane-potential or input-current domain, gates and scale/shift modulation, concatenation plus a spiking projection, and fusion at different depths. This note does not repeat the cross-attention survey (`reports/NSMT 결합부 spiking attention 후보.md` and `research_notes/NSMT 결합부 spiking attention 후보/`). Where that survey already covered a paper (CMA, S-CMRL), only the new material is given here. Only papers whose full text I opened are used as evidence.

How each claim was checked. Full texts were read as arXiv, Frontiers or Nature PDFs converted with pdftotext. Numbers were copied from the tables. Venues were checked through Crossref DOI records, the arXiv comment field, the PDF header, or the mlanthology/OpenReview listing, as stated per item. **[arXiv-only]** marks items with no peer-reviewed venue found. "Paper-measured" means a number the paper reports. "Inference" means my reasoning about our model; it has not been tested. Almost every number below comes from a single training run per setting unless the item says otherwise (Ceolini: 3-fold CV; XOResNet: mean ± sd; DH-SNN: 10 repeats on the XOR task). None of the papers ran on ETT, MIT-BIH or any regression benchmark except SpikeSTAG and TS-LIF (Metr-la, PEMS-BAY, Solar, Electricity).

## Context recap (from the assignment and the earlier report, not from sources)

- Hippocampus spikes H ∈ {0,1}^{T_h=4 × B × N × D}. Neocortex spikes Z ∈ {0,1}^{N × B × 1 × D}, one vector per token, read sequentially (token i depends on tokens ≤ i). Both index the same N patch tokens, so a token-aligned operator needs only a reshape Z → [1, B, N, D] and a copy over the 4 steps.
- Current coupling: softmax-free spiking cross-attention (q from Z copied over 4 steps; k, v from H; learned relative-position bias) → y = LIF(BN(Linear(LIF(z)))) → IAND gate x ← x·(1 − y).
- Our measurements: zeroing the Neocortex output at test time changes forecasting MSE by < 1 %; about 97 % of q·k products are 0. On ETT (11-08 probe, before Neocortex MS), Neocortex last-layer firing was 2–4 %, the first token never fired, and 70–83 % of (row, token) positions had an all-zero Neocortex vector.

## Cross-cutting findings

**F1. Our IAND gate makes "ignore the Neocortex" the easiest solution.** Paper-measured: SEW chose IAND, g(A, S) = (1 − A)·S, because identity mapping is reached by A ≡ 0. That is easy to learn: set the last BN to zero. AND needs A ≡ 1, which is hard to hold at every step, and caused a "silence problem" (DVS Gesture 70.49 % vs 95.49 % for IAND) ([SEW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04159)). Inference: our gate x ← x·(1 − y) is the same IAND with A = y. If y never fires, the Hippocampus passes unchanged and the Neocortex has no effect. A silent y is also the state that surrogate gradients reinforce, since gradients vanish when the y neuron's input is far below threshold. Also, ∂out/∂y = −x, which is 0 wherever the Hippocampus spike x is 0. FiLM- or adaLN-style modulation initialized to identity (γ = 1, β = 0) has the same "ignore" default. Operators in which the Neocortex term always enters the neuron input (current ADD, concat + projection, a convex gate initialized at 0.5) have no free "ignore" solution. They reach it only if the learned weight α or W_n is driven to zero.

**F2. Merging currents beats merging spikes in the papers that compared them.** Paper-measured: XOResNet found that merging non-identity shortcuts as currents (ADD before the neuron) beat merging output spikes with OR at every depth on Fashion-MNIST (90.59 vs 91.28 % at 11 layers; 93.88 vs 94.11 % at 50 layers), "indicating that current merging proves more effective than spike merging" ([XOResNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30362)). CIS-Fuse beat element-wise add and concat + 1×1 conv on most metrics by combining the two modalities' currents before thresholding ([CIS-Fuse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19879)). TS-LIF adds a direct current path into the soma, so the soma no longer gets input only through the dendrite. With that path it beat TC-LIF on 6/6 dataset-horizon cells in its Table 8 ([TS-LIF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05108)). Inference: the Neocortex reaches the output through a long serial chain (Z → q → q·k → z → y → gate). A direct current path from Z into the Hippocampus neurons is the "somatic shortcut" analogue.

**F3. Head-to-head operator comparisons inside SNNs are small and inconsistent, except that spike OR is worst and FiLM / plain gates can hurt.**

**F4. When one stream dominates, the fusion operator alone did not fix it.** Paper-measured: TAAF reports that "without the TAAF module … the modality dominance remains unchanged during the whole training process" (audio dominated CREMA-D training), and that its loss-side balancing moved accuracy by 1.0–3.2 points ([TAAF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14535)). IEMF raised SNN accuracy with every fusion operator it tried by scaling only the fusion module's gradient ([IEMF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10176)). Ceolini et al. trained each stream to be informative on its own first, then trained only the fusion layer ([Ceolini](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2020.00637)). Inference: our symptom ("Neocortex output can be zeroed at test time with < 1 % MSE change") is the classic modality-imbalance symptom. An operator swap should be paired with at least one training-side check: a Neocortex-only auxiliary head, or occasional Hippocampus dropout.

## Method entries

### 1. SEW element-wise functions: ADD, AND, IAND

- **Paper.** "Deep Residual Learning in Spiking Neural Networks", Wei Fang, Zhaofei Yu, Yanqi Chen, Tiejun Huang, Timothée Masquelier, Yonghong Tian. NeurIPS 2021 (arXiv comment "Accepted by NeurIPS 2021"). arXiv 2102.04159. Code in SpikingJelly.
- **How the two tensors combine.** O[t] = g(A[t], S[t]), where A = SN(F(S)) is the residual-branch spike tensor and S the shortcut spike tensor. ADD: A + S. AND: A·S. IAND: (1 − A)·S (their Table 1). AND and IAND give binary output (logic only). ADD gives {0,1,2}, so the next layer's input is no longer binary.
- **Evidence (paper-measured).** DVS Gesture, 7B-Net (Table 4): SEW ADD 97.92 %, SEW IAND 95.49 %, plain net 91.67 %, Spiking ResNet 90.97 %, SEW AND 70.49 %. They attribute AND's failure to a "silence problem": O ≤ input, so deep AND blocks go silent. An independent re-run inside SynA-ResNet (21 layers; their Table III) gives a different order. DVS Gesture: ADD-SEW 96.18 %, AND-SEW 97.22 %, IAND-SEW 96.88 %. CIFAR10-DVS: ADD-SEW 72.85 %, AND-SEW 78.12 %, IAND-SEW 77.34 %. The ranking is not stable.
- **Cost.** 0 parameters. Under SynA's accounting, ADD-SEW needed 4,462 M MACs and 21.3 mJ on DVS Gesture, vs 840 M MACs and 4.8–4.9 mJ for AND/IAND-SEW. The integer outputs of ADD turn downstream synaptic operations into MACs ([SynA-ResNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06570)).
- **Fit (inference).** Shapes match after broadcasting Z over the 4 steps. Plain logic between Z and H does not help us. Z is 0 at 70–83 % of ETT positions, so AND would erase almost everything, OR would add almost nothing, and IAND driven by Z is our current failure mode (F1). Most useful as an explanation and as a cheap control: x ← x ∨ Z_i (OR) or x ← x + Z_i (ADD) at the coupling point tests whether the Neocortex spikes carry anything when nothing can switch them off.
- **Concerns.** Residual-block study on vision; no two-modality test; single runs; operator ranking flips between papers.

### 2. OR, XOR and OR-ADD merges (ORRC / SynA-ResNet; XOResNet)

- **Papers.** (a) "SynA-ResNet: Spike-driven ResNet Achieved through OR Residual Connection", Yimeng Shan, Xuerui Qiu, Rui-Jie Zhu, Jason K. Eshraghian, Malu Zhang, Haicheng Qu. **[arXiv-only]** 2311.06570 (v3 2024; IEEE-style manuscript; no venue found in Crossref or web search). (b) "XOResNet: Exclusive-OR Meta-Residuals Facilitate Deep Spiking Neural Networks Learning", Jianfang Wu, Junsong Wang. Neurocomputing 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.neucom.2026.134933 (Crossref); arXiv 2605.30362.
- **How the two tensors combine.** OR: x + y − x·y (binary). XOR: x + y − 2xy (binary; marks where the two branches disagree). XOResNet's OA shortcut uses OR on spikes for identity shortcuts and adds currents before the neuron for non-identity shortcuts. Its "XOR meta-residual" is MR = f₁(S) ⊕ f₂(S), so the backbone learns only what differs from the shortcut. SynA-ResNet adds an attention module ("SynA") that filters the OR shortcut.
- **Evidence (paper-measured).** XOResNet Table 4 (Fashion-MNIST, mean ± sd). Depth 11: OR/OR 90.59 ± 0.08 %, OR/current-ADD 91.28 ± 0.03 %, + XOR meta-residual 92.98 ± 0.04 %. Depth 50: 93.88, 94.11, 94.53 %. SynA-ResNet Table III: plain OR-Spiking ResNet 95.83 % on DVS Gesture and 69.92 % on CIFAR10-DVS, the worst of the residual variants on CIFAR10-DVS. With SynA, 97.92 % / 78.51 %. They also report "natural pruning": after training with SynA, some or all shortcuts stop contributing.
- **Cost.** OR/XOR: 0 parameters, logic only. OA's current ADD is an addition before the neuron.
- **Fit (inference).** XOR(Z_i, H_{t,i}) is a binary "novelty" signal: channels where the Neocortex summary and the current Hippocampus token disagree. It needs no weights, but with Z mostly 0 it reduces to H. The useful lesson is the OR-vs-current comparison (F2). SynA's "natural pruning" is a warning: a learned filter on one branch can learn to drop that branch, which is the same failure we see.
- **Concerns.** Residual merges inside one network, not two streams; vision only; SynA-ResNet unrefereed.

### 3. Gated current injection before the threshold (CIS operator)

- **Paper.** "Current Injection Spiking Neural Network for Infrared and Visible Image Fusion" (CIS-Fuse), Rui Zhao, Zhuoyuan Li, Wenrui Li, Yanchen Dong, Yajing Zheng, et al. **[arXiv-only]** 2607.19879 (July 2026; "code will be released upon publication").
- **How the two tensors combine (Eqs. 6–9).** Both feature maps are spike maps from a shared spiking encoder. I_main = BN(Conv3×3(F_main)), I_aux = BN(Conv3×3(F_aux)), G = Sigmoid(BN(Conv1×1(F_aux))). The fused current is I_cis = I_main + α ⊙ G ⊙ I_aux, with α ∈ R^C a learnable per-channel injection strength. I_cis drives one PLIF neuron: H = F(V, I_cis), S = Θ(H − V_th). Output F_fuse = SN(BN(Conv3×3(S))) + F_main. In the authors' words, "an auxiliary response that is insufficient to trigger a spike independently can still shift the membrane potential". Two CIS modules alternate directions (IR → VIS, then VIS → IR on the updated IR feature) in a "BCMF" block. Only the auxiliary current is gated ("driver-modulator asymmetry"). Spike-only? No: G is a sigmoid and α ⊙ G ⊙ I_aux are real multiplies. With G ≡ 1 and α folded into BN, it reduces to a spike-only current ADD.
- **Evidence (paper-measured, MSRS test set, Table II, one setting per variant; higher is better).** Element-wise add: EN 6.60, SD 40.89, SF 10.88, AG 3.65, SCD 1.80, VIFF 0.89. Concat + 1×1 conv: 6.61, 41.73, 10.49, 3.48, 1.82, 0.85. CIS: 6.69, 43.70, 11.30, 3.74, 1.86, 0.90. CC is 0.62–0.63 for all three. One-direction injection only (IR→VIS or VIS→IR): SD 41.05 / 40.70 vs 43.70 for both directions. The learned α of the two branches settled at means 0.923 and 0.532 (Fig. 7). Network-wide firing rate ≈ 14.5 %.
- **Cost.** Per CIS module: two 3×3 conv-BN, one 1×1 conv-BN, α. Energy is counted with the 0.9 pJ AC / 4.6 pJ MAC convention, scaled by firing rate. The text does not say whether the gate multiplications are counted. My count for our D = 64: W_n (64×64) + gate W_g (64×64) + α (64) ≈ 8.3 k parameters per fusion point. The current coupling attention has 4 projections ≈ 16.4 k weights plus the bias table. Z is identical over the 4 steps, so W_n·Z, G and α ⊙ G ⊙ I_aux can be computed once per token and reused.
- **Fit (inference).** Best structural match. (i) It is token-aligned: inject the current from Z_i into the neurons that process H_{·,i}; no pairwise q·k product, so sparse spikes on both sides no longer multiply to zero. (ii) A constant injected current over 4 steps shifts the Hippocampus neuron's spike count (0/1/2/4 spikes for rising current, per the LIF table in the earlier report), so the Neocortex acts as a per-token, per-channel gain or threshold shift. (iii) There is no free "ignore" solution unless α → 0, and α can be monitored or lower-bounded. Two placements: replace "attention + IAND" by x_i ← LIF(BN(W_h x_i) + α ⊙ G_i ⊙ BN(W_n Z_i)), or inject into an existing Hippocampus LIF (attention-output or MLP-hidden neuron) as an extra current. Spike-only variant: G ≡ 1. Membrane variant: inject the Neocortex last-layer membrane potential instead of its spikes. That needs real-valued MACs, but it sidesteps the silent-Neocortex problem, because spikes are 0 at 70–83 % of ETT positions and W_n·Z there contributes only BN's constant offset.
- **Concerns.** arXiv-only; image fusion quality metrics, not prediction; single runs; gains of 0.04–2.8 SD units. The gate adds real multiplies. The injected current carries information only where the Neocortex fires (or use its membrane). Also untested whether the stronger stream then absorbs the injected current as a bias.

### 4. Dendritic-branch current integration (DH-LIF) and direct somatic injection (TS-LIF)

- **Papers.** (a) "Temporal dendritic heterogeneity incorporated with spiking neural networks for learning multi-timescale dynamics", Hanle Zheng, Zhong Zheng, Rui Hu, Bo Xiao, Yujie Wu, Fangwen Yu, Xue Liu, Guoqi Li, Lei Deng. Nature Communications 15:277 (2024), DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-44614-z (Crossref). (b) "TS-LIF: A Temporal Segment Spiking Neuron Network for Time Series Forecasting", Shibo Feng, Wanjin Feng, Xingyu Gao, Peilin Zhao, Zhiqi Shen. ICLR 2025 (PDF header "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2025"); arXiv 2503.05108.
- **How the two tensors combine.** DH-LIF (Eqs. 5–8): each dendritic branch d keeps its own current i_d[t+1] = α_d i_d[t] + (1 − α_d) I_d[t+1], where I_d = W_d·x + U_d·o comes only from the synapses wired to branch d (sparse masks). The soma integrates the sum: u[t+1] = β u[t] + (1 − β) Σ_d i_d[t+1] − u_th o[t]. Two input streams can be wired to two branches with different learned timescales α_d. TS-LIF (Eqs. 5–6): a dendrite compartment and a soma compartment are coupled (β terms). The input current also enters the soma directly ("direct somatic current injection", (1 − α₂)c[t]). Both compartments spike, and the output is s_mix = κ s_d + (1 − κ) s_s with learnable per-channel κ. Spike-only? Branch currents are weighted spike sums with constant decays (like an LIF leak). s_mix with real κ yields non-binary output unless κ is folded into the next layer's weights.
- **Evidence (paper-measured).** DH-SNN multi-timescale spiking XOR: Signal 1 (slow) and Signal 2 (fast) are wired to Branch 1 and Branch 2 of each neuron. The vanilla SFNN scored "much lower than 75 %". One-branch DH-SFNNs "cannot process information with multiple timescales well". Two-branch DH-SFNNs did much better, especially with slow/fast initialization (bar-chart values only; 10 repeats). Random wiring of the two signals to the two branches also worked (Supplementary Fig. S4). TS-LIF Table 8 (GRU backbone, vs TC-LIF, which has the dendrite→soma path but no direct injection): Metr-la horizon 6 R² 0.848 vs 0.828, horizon 96 0.329 vs 0.259; Electricity horizon 96 0.976 vs 0.941. Seed sd reported in their Table 10 (3 seeds).
- **Cost.** DH-LIF: an extra state and decay per branch. Connection masks keep the parameter count constant as branches grow. TS-LIF: two compartments plus κ. For us, a Neocortex branch adds W_n (64×64 ≈ 4.1 k) plus per-neuron α_d.
- **Fit (inference).** This is the bio-inspired version of #3 and matches the CLS idea. Give each Hippocampus neuron two dendritic branches: a fast branch for its own input and a slow branch for the Neocortex current Z_i. The soma sums both before threshold, so no product of sparse spikes is needed. The slow branch can also hold the Neocortex signal across the 4 steps and across tokens. TS-LIF is evidence, in forecasting, that a direct current path into the firing compartment helps relative to a serial path, which is the shape of our long Z → q → … → gate chain.
- **Concerns.** Both are neuron models tested with one input stream, except the synthetic XOR. Neither tests two learned deep streams. DH-SNN numbers are in figures. TS-LIF used no ETT (its datasets are Metr-la, PEMS-BAY, Solar, Electricity).

### 5. Conductance (shunting) gating: Dynamic Gated Neuron

- **Paper.** "A Brain-Inspired Gating Mechanism Unlocks Robust Computation in Spiking Neural Networks", Qianyi Bai, Haiteng Wang, Qiang Yu. ICLR 2026 (mlanthology; OpenReview id 5h741EyfQM); arXiv 2509.03281.
- **How it combines inputs (Eqs. 5–7).** Synaptic trace D_i^t = e^{−Δt/τ_s} D_i^{t−1} + z_i^t. Leak factor ρ_t = φ(1 − g_l Δt − Δt Σ_i C_i D_i^t). Membrane V^t = ρ_t V^{t−1} + Δt Σ_i W_i D_i^t − ϑ z^{t−1}. Input spikes therefore do two things: drive current (W) and open a leak conductance (C) that multiplies V. The authors compare this to an LSTM forget gate. Spike-only? No: ρ_t·V is a data-dependent multiply.
- **Evidence (paper-measured, single stream, compared with literature numbers).** SHD feedforward: DGN 85.18 % vs TC-LIF 83.08 %. SHD recurrent: DGN 87.78 % vs TC-LIF 88.91 % (lower). TIDIGITS recurrent 99.10 %. Strong gains under input noise and adversarial perturbations.
- **Fit (inference).** As a fusion operator: let the Neocortex spikes Z_i drive the conductance term C·D of the Hippocampus neurons while H drives the current term W·D. The Neocortex then controls how long the Hippocampus neuron remembers (a multiplicative "forget" gate) rather than what it receives. Unlike the IAND gate it acts on the membrane, not on output spikes, so it changes timing even when the neuron would not have fired.
- **Concerns.** Never used to fuse two streams; single-stream evidence only; data-dependent multiplies; only 4 Hippocampus steps for a leak gate to act on. Lowest priority of the membrane-domain options.

### 6. Firing-rate cross-gating with concatenation (CMA)

- **Paper.** "Enhancing SNN-based spatio-temporal learning: A benchmark dataset and Cross-Modality Attention model", Shibo Zhou, Bo Yang, Mengwen Yuan, Runhao Jiang, Rui Yan, Gang Pan, Huajin Tang. Neural Networks 180:106677 (2024), DOI 10.1016/j.neunet.2024.106677 (Crossref); arXiv 2410.15689. The mechanism was already in the earlier note (Q2). The fusion-strategy numbers below were not extracted there.
- **How it combines (Eqs. 11–13).** The event stream's firing rate per time step gives temporal scores d_e = σ(M₂ δ(M₁ r_e)). The frame stream's firing-rate map gives spatial scores d_f = σ(conv3×3(r_f)). Cross-application: frame spikes are scaled per step by the event-derived score (s̃_f(t) = d_e(t)·s_f(t)), and event spikes are scaled per location by the frame-derived score. The two results are concatenated, [s̃_e; s̃_f]. No query-key products. Spike-only? No: real scores multiply spikes, so the output is real-valued.
- **Evidence (paper-measured, DVS-SLR, Table 7, 9 latency settings).** At 0.2 s, dt = 10 ms, T = 20: early concat 76.54, middle concat 77.43, late spike-OR 72.92, late probability average 75.83, Liu et al. 2022 modality-attention fusion 79.67, CMA 83.67 %. Uni-modal: event 70.49 %, frame 64.71 %. My computation over the 9 settings: CMA minus the better of early/middle concat ranges from +4.4 to +8.1 points. Late OR is the lowest, or tied lowest, in most settings. They also report CMA beats middle concat for LIF, IF, PLIF and LIAF neurons (Table 10, from the earlier note).
- **Cost.** A small MLP and conv on firing rates; doubles the channel count by concatenation.
- **Fit (inference).** Natural mapping: the Neocortex reads tokens in order, so it can supply a per-token (and per-channel) gain g_i = σ(MLP(Z_i or Neocortex membrane)) for H_{·,i}: the "when" signal. The Hippocampus can supply a per-channel gain back. Then concatenate and project. The gain multiplies Hippocampus spikes, so it acts only where H fires. Unlike IAND it can amplify, not just erase.
- **Concerns.** Classification of video-like data; single runs; the comparison "AF" baseline is a different paper's module. Real-valued multiplies. If the Neocortex is silent, its rate is 0 and the gain becomes σ(bias), a constant.

### 7. Convex sigmoid gate between a sequential stream and a spiking self-attention stream (SpikeSTAG DSF)

- **Paper.** "SpikeSTAG: Spatial-Temporal Forecasting via GNN-SNN Collaboration", Bang Hu, Changze Lv, Mingjie Li, Yunpeng Liu, Xiaoqing Zheng, Fengzhe Zhang, Wei Cao, Fan Zhang. **[arXiv-only]** 2508.02069 (Aug 2025; no venue found).
- **How it combines.** The spike output of a graph block feeds a lightweight LSTM (continuous hidden states) and a spiking self-attention (SSA) branch. Fusion: G = σ(W[H_LSTM; H_SSA]), H_fused = G ⊙ H_LSTM + (1 − G) ⊙ H_SSA (a highway/GRU-style convex gate). Spike-only? No: sigmoid, real multiplies, and the LSTM stream is real-valued.
- **Evidence (paper-measured, Table 3; W1 LSTM only, W2 SSA only, W3 "concatenation without gating", W4 gate).** R²: Metr-la 0.823 / 0.854 / 0.858 / 0.868; PEMS-BAY 0.831 / 0.860 / 0.854 / 0.874; Solar 0.950 / 0.963 / 0.964 / 0.973; Electricity 0.960 / 0.965 / 0.966 / 0.987. RSE, W3 → W4: 0.412 → 0.375, 0.413 → 0.384, 0.251 → 0.246, 0.214 → 0.207. My computation: −9.0, −7.0, −2.0, −3.3 % relative RSE. On PEMS-BAY, concat (W3) was below SSA alone (W2). The text calls the traffic gain "1.0–1.4 %" of R², but the table gives +2.3 % relative on PEMS-BAY. The text and caption also describe W3 inconsistently (serial processing vs concatenation). Energy: 4.39 mJ for SpikeSTAG vs 3.19 mJ for iSpikformer (Electricity, L = 3).
- **Cost.** W ∈ R^{D×2D}; for D = 64 that is 8.2 k parameters per fusion point.
- **Fit (inference).** The closest published setting to ours: one stream processes the sequence recurrently, the other with spiking self-attention, the task is multivariate forecasting, and fusion is non-attention. Token-aligned version: G_i = σ(W[H̄_i; Z_i]), out_i = G_i ⊙ H_i + (1 − G_i) ⊙ P Z_i (P a projection), with the gate bias initialized so G ≈ 0.5. The model then starts by mixing rather than ignoring (F1). G gives a direct, inspectable measure of Neocortex use (mean 1 − G).
- **Concerns.** arXiv-only, one run, inconsistent ablation description. The recurrent stream is a real-valued LSTM, not a spiking Neocortex. Gate collapse to G ≈ 1 (ignore the Neocortex) is possible and should be logged. TAAF found plain gated fusion worse than concat in SNN audio-visual classification (#8).

### 8. Concat, sum, FiLM and gated fusion compared inside one SNN, with temporal weighting and modality balancing (TAAF)

- **Paper.** "Spiking Neural Networks with Temporal Attention-Guided Adaptive Fusion for imbalanced Multi-modal Learning", Jiangrong Shen, Yulin Xie, Qi Xu, Gang Pan, Huajin Tang, Badong Chen. ACM Multimedia 2025, DOI 10.1145/3746027.3755622 (Crossref); arXiv 2505.14535.
- **How it combines.** Two spiking ResNet-18 branches, a convolutional time-alignment module, then fusion F(h_m1, h_m2) by concatenation or weighted summation. A temporal module computes per-step importance scores α_u(t) with softmax over steps (Eqs. 5–7). These weight the per-step loss (Eq. 8), and per-modality gradient modulation scales each branch's learning rate. The temporal scores are attention over time steps, not cross-stream q·k. FiLM and "gated" fusion appear in the tables but are not defined in the paper.
- **Evidence (paper-measured, Table 2, AVE / CREMA-D accuracy %).** Loss-adjusted SNN: concat 69.65 / 75.27, sum 69.41 / 74.60, FiLM 66.41 / 65.72, gated 63.68 / 68.01. Plus time alignment: concat 69.90 / 75.13, sum 69.15 / 75.67, FiLM 64.68 / 66.67, gated 67.66 / 68.82. Full TAAF: concat 70.65 / 77.55, sum 68.91 / 76.75. TAAF without time alignment: concat 69.90 / 76.75, sum 69.15 / **79.17**. That sum value is higher than the full model, which the paper does not explain. The training curves show audio dominating training, and "without the TAAF module … the modality dominance remains unchanged during the whole training process". Energy "18.5 MJ vs 49.91 MJ" (units as printed).
- **Fit (inference).** The most direct in-SNN evidence that FiLM-style scale/shift and plain gates can be 3–10 points worse than concat or sum, and that dominance by one stream is the main failure. It argues for concat or sum as the base operator plus a balancing method, rather than a clever operator alone.
- **Concerns.** Classification with ResNet branches; FiLM/gated implementation unknown; single runs; internal inconsistency noted above.

### 9. Late concatenation after separate per-stream training (Ceolini et al.; VT-SNN)

- **Papers.** (a) "Hand-Gesture Recognition Based on EMG and Event-Based Camera Sensor Fusion: A Benchmark in Neuromorphic Computing", Enea Ceolini, Charlotte Frenkel, Sumit Bam Shrestha, Gemma Taverni, Lyes Khacef, Melika Payvand, Elisa Donati. Frontiers in Neuroscience 14:637 (2020), DOI 10.3389/fnins.2020.00637 (Crossref). (b) "Event-Driven Visual-Tactile Sensing and Learning for Robots", Tasbolat Taunyazov, Weicong Sng, Hian Hian See, Brian Lim, Jethro Kuan, Abdul Fatir Ansari, Benjamin C.K. Tee, Harold Soh. RSS 2020, DOI 10.15607/rss.2020.xvi.020 (Crossref); arXiv 2009.07083.
- **How it combines.** Ceolini: train the EMG spiking MLP and the DVS spiking CNN separately (30 epochs each), drop their output layers, concatenate the two penultimate spike layers, and train only a new 5-way output layer (640 → 5 on Loihi). VT-SNN: tactile encoder (50 outputs) and vision encoder (10 outputs) spike trains are concatenated into one dense spiking layer, trained jointly (SLAYER). Note the deliberately narrow vision width. Spike-only: yes.
- **Evidence (paper-measured).** Ceolini Table 1 (3-fold CV). Loihi spiking CNN: EMG 55.7 ± 2.7 %, DVS 92.1 ± 1.2 %, EMG+DVS 96.0 ± 0.4 %; energy per inference 173 / 815 / 1,105 µJ. ODIN + MorphIC spiking MLP: 53.6 / 85.1 / 89.4 %; energy 7.4 / 57.2 / 37.4 µJ. VT-SNN Table I (container + weight): tactile 0.71, vision 0.73, combined 0.81. Rotational slip: 0.82 / 1.00 / 1.00.
- **Fit (inference).** Two lessons for us. (i) A weak stream (EMG at 56 %) still added +3.9 points when it had been trained to be informative on its own, and the fusion layer could not shut it out cheaply. (ii) Concatenating [H_{t,i}; Z_i] → Linear(2D → D) → BN → LIF gives the Neocortex its own weights in the post-fusion neuron (8.2 k parameters for D = 64, AC-only on spikes). A staged schedule (pretrain the Neocortex with its own forecasting head, then train the fusion) is the cheapest test of whether the Neocortex carries usable information at all.
- **Concerns.** Classification; late fusion only; frozen branches in Ceolini. A noisy weak stream can also hurt: in El-Assal et al. (STDP two-stream, Signal Processing: Image Communication 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.image.2025.117263; arXiv 2306.13783), Weizmann frame-subtraction stream 71.23 % → fused 63.45 %.

### 10. Mutual-information-weighted sum (SMF in MK-SGN)

- **Paper.** "MK-SGN: A Spiking Graph Convolutional Network with Multimodal Fusion and Knowledge Distillation for Skeleton-based Action Recognition", Naichuan Zheng, Hailun Xia, Zeyu Liang, Yuchen Du. Neurocomputing 662 (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.neucom.2025.131796 (Crossref and arXiv DOI field); arXiv 2404.10210.
- **How it combines.** A spike-form mutual-information network estimates pairwise MI between the four skeleton modalities' spike tensors. The normalized MI matrix W_MI weights the modalities: P_fuse = W_MI ∗ [P_b, P_j, P_bm, P_jm] (Eq. 13). This is early fusion. The weights are real scalars.
- **Evidence (paper-measured, NTU-RGB+D XSub, Table 7).** SMF 76.1 % (2.17 M parameters, 0.596 mJ), spiking attention 76.0 % (2.24 M, 0.613 mJ), concat 75.4 % (1.90 M, 0.589 mJ), sum 75.2 % (1.89 M, 0.579 mJ). The text swaps the attention and concat accuracies.
- **Fit (inference).** Low. A per-stream scalar weight cannot make a silent stream informative. Included as further evidence that operator choice moves accuracy by ≤ 1 point in SNN fusion.
- **Concerns.** Four homogeneous skeleton modalities; single runs.

### 11. Fusion depth: early vs middle vs late (Bjorndahl et al.; CMA Table 7)

- **Paper.** "Digit Recognition using Multimodal Spiking Neural Networks", William Bjorndahl, Jack Easton, Austin Modoff, Eric C. Larson, Joseph Camp, Prasanna Rangarajan. **[arXiv-only]** 2409.00552 (4 pages, "submitted to ICASSP 2025").
- **How it combines.** N-MNIST (visual) and SHD (audio) LIF branches, concatenated at the input, a middle layer, or just before the output. The readout is the summed softmax of output membrane potentials.
- **Evidence (paper-measured).** Visual only 92.25 %, audio only 95.29 %, early 97.35 %, middle 97.45 %, late 98.43 %. McNemar tests between depths: p = 0.473, 1.00, 0.720 (no significant difference). CMA Table 7 (#6): middle concat beat early concat in 6/9 settings by up to 2 points, and both beat late OR in most settings.
- **Fit (inference).** Our coupling is already a mid-depth, per-block fusion. These results suggest that moving the fusion point is unlikely to matter as much as the operator's "ignore" default and stream balance.
- **Concerns.** Synthetic pairing of unrelated datasets; arXiv-only.

## Training-side companions for an ignored stream (SNN papers)

- **IEMF (inverse-effectiveness driven fusion).** "Incorporating brain-inspired mechanisms for multimodal learning in artificial intelligence", Xiang He, Dongcheng Zhao, Yang Li, Qingqun Kong, Xin Yang, Yi Zeng. Science Advances 12 (2026), DOI 10.1126/sciadv.ady8751 (Crossref); arXiv 2505.10176; code Brain-Cog-Lab/IEMF. Mechanism (Eqs. 10–11): ξ_t = γ(1 + tanh(1 − S^{a−v}/S^{av})) ∈ (0, 2γ), computed from unimodal vs fused evidence scores. It multiplies only the fusion module's gradient (W_f ← W_f − η ξ_t ∇L), so the fusion weights learn faster when unimodal evidence is weak. Paper-measured SNN example: Kinetics-Sounds with the LFM fusion method went from 54.63 % to 63.53 %. Gains were reported for concat, MSLR, OGM-GE and LFM on three datasets, with lower training compute. Inference: it is the only method here that targets "fusion module learns too little" without changing the operator. For us it would need a Neocortex-only and a Hippocampus-only evidence score, e.g. from auxiliary heads.
- **TAAF loss adjustment** (#8): per-modality gradient modulation plus per-step loss weights. Paper-measured +1.0 to +3.2 points over loss-adjustment-only variants.
- **Staged per-stream training** (Ceolini, #9).
- Inference, not from a paper I opened: dropping the Hippocampus input to the fusion point with small probability during training (a modality-dropout regularizer) would force the Neocortex path to carry the prediction sometimes.

## Fit to our model and the three best candidates (inference)

What a replacement must do, given our measurements: (a) let Neocortex information reach the Hippocampus neurons without a product of two sparse spike tensors; (b) remove the zero-cost "ignore" solution of the IAND gate; (c) keep token alignment (Neocortex token i ↔ Hippocampus token i), which no current coupling term exploits directly; (d) cope with a Neocortex that is silent at 70–83 % of ETT positions. No operator fixes (d). Either repair the Neocortex front end first (as the earlier report also concluded), or feed the fusion the Neocortex membrane potential instead of its spikes, accepting real-valued MACs.

1. **Current-domain injection, CIS-style (#3), with DH-LIF branches as the bio-inspired variant (#4).** Replace "attention + IAND" with x_i ← LIF(BN(W_h x_i) + α ⊙ BN(W_n Z_i)), first with no gate (spike-only, α per channel, initialized away from 0), then with CIS's gate G_i = σ(BN(W_g Z_i)). Why: it is the only family with an in-SNN ablation beating both add and concat (CIS-Fuse), and with supporting results that merging currents beats merging spikes (XOResNet, TS-LIF). It addresses (a), (b) and (c) directly. A constant per-token current over the 4 steps becomes a per-token gain or threshold shift on the Hippocampus spike count. Cost is about half the current coupling's projection parameters. Risks: CIS-Fuse is arXiv-only image fusion with small gains; α may still shrink to 0 (log it, as CIS-Fuse Fig. 7 does).
2. **Concat + spiking projection, together with a training-side balance check (#9 with #8 and the IEMF companion).** [H_{t,i}; Z_i] → Linear(2D → D) → BN → LIF, plus one of: a Neocortex-only auxiliary forecasting head, staged training, or occasional Hippocampus dropout at the fusion point. Why: concat was the most reliable simple operator in SNN head-to-heads (TAAF: concat ≥ sum > FiLM/gated; CMA: concat > late OR; MK-SGN: concat ≥ sum), it is spike-only, and the imbalance literature (TAAF, IEMF, Ceolini) shows the stream-dominance problem must be attacked in training, not only in the operator. It is also the cleanest control. If concat plus balancing still shows < 1 % sensitivity to zeroing the Neocortex, the Neocortex probably carries little ETT-relevant information, and no operator will fix that.
3. **Convex gate between the streams, SpikeSTAG-style (#7), or CMA-style rate cross-gain (#6).** out_i = G_i ⊙ H_i + (1 − G_i) ⊙ P Z_i with G_i = σ(W[H̄_i; Z_i]) initialized near 0.5, or a Neocortex-derived per-token gain on H. Why: the only non-attention fusion evaluated in SNN forecasting with a sequential stream plus a spiking self-attention stream (SpikeSTAG: gate beat concat on 4/4 datasets, RSE −2 to −9 %). CMA shows +4 to +8 points over concat in a two-stream SNN. G is also a direct readout of Neocortex use. Risks: real multiplies; SpikeSTAG is arXiv-only with an inconsistent ablation description; TAAF found plain gated fusion worse than concat, so it ranks third.

Fair-test notes (inference). The Spikformer-style comparison model has the same IAND gate, driven by self-attention. Swap its gate for the same operator, fed by its self-attention output, so that "operator change" and "Neocortex effect" can be separated, as the earlier report required for attention changes. Log per run: firing rate of Z and of the fused neurons; the fraction of fused-neuron spikes that flip when Z (or its current) is zeroed at test time; ΔMSE for Z → 0 at test time; the learned α or mean gate (1 − G); and the ratio of gradient norms reaching the Neocortex vs the Hippocampus. Keep the existing judgment rule (12 pairs, R ≤ −1 % and ≥ 8/12). Count the new fusion operations explicitly in energy: sigmoid and gate multiplies as MACs. W_n·Z can be computed once per token, since Z is identical over the 4 steps.
