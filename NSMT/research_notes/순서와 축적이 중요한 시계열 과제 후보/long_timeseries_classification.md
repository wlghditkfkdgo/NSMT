## Q1. What is the true published picture on CinC 2017 (scores, metric, small models, SNNs)?

### Takeaway
The lab's memory of "80 %+" is correct only for the official metric and setting: mean F1 over Normal, AF and Other (Noisy excluded), on the hidden test set, mostly from ensembles or hand-crafted features (winners 0.825–0.831). Single deep networks evaluated by 5-fold cross-validation on the public 8,528 records score 74.6–79.2 on that same 3-class metric, and their Noisy-class F1 is only 55–65, so the lab's ~66 % four-class macro-F1 is not comparable to "80 %+" and the real gap to a 24-layer CNN/CRNN is several points, not fifteen.

### Cited Findings
- Data: 12,186 single-lead ECGs from an AliveCor hand-held device, 300 Hz, 9–61 s; 8,528 public training records and 3,658 hidden test records; the test set "has not been made available to the public" (Sec. 2.1) — [Clifford et al., CinC 2017](https://www.physionet.org/files/challenge-2017/1.0.0/papers/065-469.pdf)
- Class counts, final labels (v3), Table 2: training Normal 5,076 (59.5 %), AF 758 (8.9 %), Other 2,415 (28.3 %), Noisy 279 (3.3 %); hidden test Normal 2,437 (66.6 %), AF 286 (7.8 %), Other 683 (18.7 %), Noisy 252 (6.9 %). The test distribution differs from the training distribution after relabelling — [Clifford et al., CinC 2017](https://www.physionet.org/files/challenge-2017/1.0.0/papers/065-469.pdf)
- Official score, Eq. 1: F1 = (F1n + F1a + F1o) / 3. The Noisy-class F1 (F1p) is defined but is not part of the score — [Clifford et al., CinC 2017](https://www.physionet.org/files/challenge-2017/1.0.0/papers/065-469.pdf)
- Final scores on the full hidden test set, Table 4: Teijeiro 0.831, Datta 0.829, Zabihi 0.826, Hong 0.825 (four joint winners, all rounded to 0.83); joint fifth Baydoun 0.822, Bin 0.821, Zihlmann 0.821, Xiong 0.818. The same entries scored 0.875–0.970 on the training data ("significant overtraining was seen even in the winning entries") — [Clifford et al., CinC 2017](https://www.physionet.org/files/challenge-2017/1.0.0/papers/065-469.pdf)
- Voting over entries, Table 4: top-10 0.844, all 75 entries 0.855, LASSO-selected 0.858, LASSO plus signal-quality features 0.868. No single model reached these — [Clifford et al., CinC 2017](https://www.physionet.org/files/challenge-2017/1.0.0/papers/065-469.pdf)
- Method types of the top entries, as given by their paper titles on the official results page: Datta — "cascaded binary classifier"; Hong — "ENCASE: an ensemble classifier … using expert features and deep neural networks"; Teijeiro — "abductive interpretation of short single-lead ECG records"; Zabihi — "random forest classifier"; Bin (0.82) — "decision tree ensemble"; Xiong (0.82) — "novel neural networks" (journal version: convolutional recurrent network); Zihlmann (0.82) — "convolutional recurrent neural networks" — [PhysioNet Challenge 2017 results](https://moody-challenge.physionet.org/2017/results)
- Organisers' reading: "a standard random forest with well chosen features performed as well as more complex approaches", suggesting 8,528 training records were not enough to give complex models an advantage; and agreement between annotators "remained low even after relabeling" (Table 1: κ 0.245 over the 1,129 most-disputed test records) — [Clifford et al., CinC 2017](https://www.physionet.org/files/challenge-2017/1.0.0/papers/065-469.pdf)
- Single deep models under 5-fold cross-validation on the public set (24-layer CNN on logarithmic spectrograms; CRNN = 24-layer CNN + 3-layer LSTM network), Table 1 with data augmentation: CNN per-class F1 N 87.8 / A 79.0 / O 70.1 / Noisy 65.3, 3-class score 79.0; CRNN 88.8 / 76.4 / 72.6 / 64.5, score 79.2. Table 2 without augmentation: CNN 88.3 / 69.9 / 69.1 / 59.6, score 75.8; CRNN 87.4 / 69.9 / 66.5 / 54.9, score 74.6. An ensemble of five CRNNs scored 0.82 on the hidden test set — [Zihlmann et al., CinC 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06122)
- SNN results on CinC 2017, all reported as accuracy rather than the challenge F1 (survey table): Feng et al. 2022 (Biomedical Signal Processing and Control), ANN-to-SNN conversion, 84.4 %; Rana et al., ANN-to-SNN with LIF neurons, 85.8 %; Banerjee et al. (STDP reservoir + logistic regression), 78.5 % — ["The Promise of Spiking Neural Networks for Ubiquitous Computing" survey, arXiv 2025, results table](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01737)
- The 85.8 % figure is confirmed in the primary abstract ("overall accuracy of 93.8 % on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia dataset and 85.8 % on the 2017 PhysioNet Challenge dataset") — [Rana et al., Sensors 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24113426)
- The same survey is internally inconsistent about Banerjee et al.: its text says "92 % accuracy on the PhysioNet 2017 dataset using a reservoir-based SNN" while its table lists 78.5 % — [SNN survey, arXiv 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01737)

### Inferences
- Four-class macro-F1 implied by Zihlmann's cross-validation tables (my arithmetic from their per-class values): 75.6 (CNN and CRNN, with augmentation), 71.7 (CNN) and 69.7 (CRNN) without augmentation. The lab's ~66 % four-class macro-F1 from a 0.2–1.5 M-parameter model is therefore about 4–10 points below a 24-layer network evaluated the same way, not ~15 points.
- Three things inflate the remembered "80 %+": (i) the metric drops the hardest class (Noisy F1 55–65); (ii) hidden-test scores come from ensembles and feature pipelines, and cross-validation scores of the same teams' single models are 3–7 points lower; (iii) the hidden test set has a different class mix (Other 18.7 % vs 28.3 %).
- No SNN paper on CinC 2017 was found in a top machine-learning venue, and none reports the challenge F1. The existing SNN numbers (78.5–85.8 % accuracy) cannot be compared with the lab's macro-F1: with 59.5 % Normal records, accuracy is dominated by the Normal class.
- CinC 2017 remains usable if the lab reports F1(N, A, O) under 5-fold cross-validation and cites Zihlmann's cross-validation rows (74.6–79.2) as the like-for-like deep baseline, with 0.83 named as the hidden-test ensemble level.
- The organisers' own conclusion (random forests on RR-interval and morphology statistics tie with deep networks) supports the lab's earlier reading that most of the label is carried by accumulated statistics rather than by order.

### Gaps
- Lightweight-model numbers were seen only in search summaries and are unverified: a dense 128-32-4 network on features with F1 ≈ 0.804 under 5-fold cross-validation ([PMC6377428](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6377428)), and an end-to-end 1-D CNN with F1 78.2 % ([PMC7180882](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180882)). PubMed Central blocked automated reading; parameter counts were not seen.
- Feng et al. 2022 (the SNN co-authored by a challenge winner) could not be opened; whether its 84.4 % uses four classes and which split is unknown.
- No post-2020 paper with a verified parameter count below 1.5 M and the challenge F1 under cross-validation was read this session.

## Q2–Q3. Candidate benchmarks: length, classes, split, metric, best DNN numbers, SNN numbers, small-model numbers

### Takeaway
Only four families have peer-reviewed SNN numbers on a shared protocol: per-time-step ECG wave labelling on QTDB (Nature MI 2021, ICML 2024, Nature Communications 2025), raw Speech Commands (ICLR 2025), short-window activity recognition (UCI-HAR / SHAR / HHAR), and — at preprint / journal level — sleep staging and EigenWorms. Rhythm-level 12-lead ECG sets (PTB-XL, CPSC 2018, Chapman) and wearable long-context activity data (CAPTURE-24) have solid DNN baselines but no SNN baselines found; seizure detection has many SNN papers but no common protocol.

ECG wave labelling on QTDB (the SNN community's own ECG sequence task)
- Task: label every time step of a two-channel ECG from the QT database as one of six wave segments (P, PQ, QR, RS, ST, TP); the input is level-crossing spike trains. First results: adaptive spiking recurrent network 84.4 % with 46 neurons, same-size LIF network 73.7 %, LSTM 78.9 %, GRU 77.3 %, vanilla RNN 74.8 %, bidirectional LSTM with 290 units 80.76 %, non-spiking ReLU version of the recurrent network 86.4 % (Table 1 and text) — [Yin, Corradi & Bohté, arXiv 2020 (later Nature Machine Intelligence 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11633)
- Follow-ups on the same data: adaptive LIF (Yin et al. 2021) 85.9 % with 1,776 parameters; resonate-and-fire 85.5 ± 0.7, balanced resonate-and-fire 85.8 ± 0.7, BHRF 87.0 ± 0.4 with 1,734 parameters; segments are 1,300 ms long (Table 1, Appendix A.7) — [Higuchi et al., ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14603)
- SE-adLIF 86.88 ± 0.40 versus a one-layer LIF network 77.8 ± 1.85, both with 1.8 K parameters (Table 2) — [Baronig et al., Nature Communications 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07517)

### Inferences
- Of the six UEA sets in the Log-NCDE table only EigenWorms and SCP1 separate models at all; the other four are at or near the majority-class rate (see Q4), so the "average over six" column is mostly noise.
- QTDB wave labelling is the only ECG task with a chain of top-venue SNN baselines, and models there have under 2 K parameters, so "small model not hopelessly below" is automatically satisfied; the cost is that there is no DNN state of the art beyond small recurrent networks.
- For sleep staging the SNN level (MF1 68–75 on Sleep-EDF) sits clearly below the DNN level (MF1 ≈ 79–80 on Sleep-EDF), which leaves room for a new SNN to beat the SNN baselines without having to match the DNNs. The two SNN papers use different subject counts and folds, so the lab would have to pick one protocol.
- PTB-XL, CPSC 2018 and Chapman have fixed folds and strong small baselines (a 2-layer LSTM is within 0.4 points of the best network on PTB-XL rhythm) but no SNN baselines; a result there would be "first SNN on the standard folds".
- CHB-MIT numbers above 94 % come from random segment-level cross-validation, where windows of the same seizure fall on both sides of the split; the leave-one-out figure for the same dataset is 89.0 % area under the curve. The SNN numbers are not comparable with each other.

### Gaps
- SHHS, ISRUC and TUH (TUSZ / TUAB) were not examined with primary sources this session; no SNN paper using the TUH standard splits was confirmed (the "Tiny dLIF" abstract names "USA adult scalp EEG" without the dataset name).
- XSleepNet's own Sleep-EDF-20 / -78 numbers appear only in a figure in the text extraction (model identity ambiguous), so they are not reported here; U-Sleep, DeepSleepNet and TinySleepNet numbers were not re-read.
- CPSC 2018 challenge-winner F1 0.837 was seen only in a search summary (from [arXiv 2406.16928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16928)); unverified.
- Lightweight sleep DNNs seen only in search summaries: ULW-SleepNet 13.3 K parameters, 86.9 % / 81.4 % ([arXiv 2602.23852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23852)); LWSleepNet 86.6 % and MF1 79.2 on Sleep-EDF-20 ([PMC10388613](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10388613)); unverified.
- WISDM, PAMAP2 and Opportunity: no primary source with a standard split and parameter counts was read; the WISDM SNN figures come from a survey table only.
- CPSC 2021 (paroxysmal AF onset / offset on long records) and Apnea-ECG were not re-read; see the earlier notes (`physiological_long_context_tasks.md`), which found no official leaderboard numbers and no SNN paper for CPSC 2021.
- Hybrid SNN for sleep EEG (Jia et al., Science China Information Sciences 2022, 63 citations in OpenAlex) has no abstract in OpenAlex; its numbers were not seen.

## Q4. What published evidence shows that order matters, and that long context matters — and where does it show the opposite?

### Takeaway
No source found this session contains a shuffled-segment control on any of the real candidate datasets, so "order matters" is nowhere demonstrated directly; what exists is evidence about context length, and it mostly points to short horizons: sleep staging gains 3–6 accuracy points from neighbouring epochs but saturates at 10–15 epochs, 12-lead ECG classification peaks at 2–3 s of input, and wrist activity recognition gains most from label smoothing across windows. For the UEA long sets, position-pooling methods (ROCKET, interval forests) equal or beat the sequence models, which is evidence against an order requirement.

### Cited Findings

Evidence against an order / long-context requirement
- UEA archive, accuracy averaged over 30 resamples, Table 11 (majority-class rate / ROCKET / InceptionTime / CIF / HIVE-COTE / ResNet): EigenWorms 42.0 / 86.28 / — / 90.33 / 78.17 / 45.45; EthanolConcentration 25.1 / 44.68 / 27.92 / 72.89 / 80.68 / 28.62 (best overall: shapelet transform 82.36, Table 10); Heartbeat 72.2 / 71.76 / 73.20 / 76.52 / 72.18 / 63.89; MotorImagery 50.0 / 53.13 / 51.17 / 51.80 / 52.17 / 49.77 (best 53.80); SCP1 50.2 / 86.55 / 84.69 / 85.94 / 86.02 / 76.11 (best: TapNet 95.68); SCP2 50.0 / 51.35 / 52.04 / 48.87 / 51.67 / 50.24 (best 53.69) — [Ruiz et al., DMKD 2021](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10618-020-00727-3.pdf)
- "ROCKET is the best ranked and by far the fastest classifier and would be our recommendation as the default choice" for multivariate problems — [Ruiz et al., DMKD 2021](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10618-020-00727-3.pdf)
- ROCKET's features are global max pooling and the proportion of positive values of random dilated convolution outputs, i.e. pooled over the whole time axis — [Dempster et al., MiniRocket, KDD 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08791)
- For AtrialFibrillation, FingerMovements, Heartbeat, MotorImagery, SelfRegulationSCP2 and possibly StandWalkJump "few, if any, classifiers do better than predicting the majority class"; for SCP2 there is "probably" no useful information "according to the original data source" — [Ruiz et al., arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13156)
- Class shares in the 2026 archive description: EigenWorms largest class 42.47 %; Heartbeat abnormal 72.13 %; MotorImagery and SCP2 50 % / 50 %. SCP2 "was introduced with the warning" that it is unclear whether it contains useful information — [Middlehurst et al., arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20352)
- EthanolConcentration is "raw spectra taken of water-and-ethanol solutions" in whisky bottles: its axis is wavelength, not time — [Ruiz et al., DMKD 2021](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10618-020-00727-3.pdf)
- Permutation test on the 112 univariate UCR sets (one random permutation of time indices applied to every series in train and test): accuracy stayed within the summed standard deviations for 38 sets (34 %) with MiniRocket and 36 (32 %) with Catch-22; within twice that margin for 55 (49 %) and 56 (50 %). The authors conclude that "a significant portion of UCR datasets does not rely on temporal information" — [Zhang, Batista & Kanhere, arXiv 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20264)
- On the univariate archive the two best methods in the 2024 re-evaluation are MultiROCKET+Hydra and HIVE-COTE v2 — [Middlehurst et al., "Bake off redux", DMKD 2024, arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13029)
- 12-lead ECG: "No long-term dependencies in ECG data beyond 3 s" — macro-AUC on PTB-XL as a function of input window "shows a peak around input sizes around 2–3 s"; models "profit more from averaging overlapping predictions from different sliding windows"; the authors add that this does not settle "long-term ECGs with potentially rapid rhythm changes" — [Mehari & Strodthoff, ML4H 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07579)
- Sleep: "no significant increase in performance upon increasing the model's input size" from 10 to 200 epochs (0.790–0.796 MF1, Table I), which "questions the diagnostic value of very long" contexts; training directly on 200 epochs was significantly worse (0.723) — [Wang & Strodthoff, arXiv 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17779)
- Short-window activity recognition is saturated (96–99 % on UCI-HAR for CNN, DeepConvLSTM and SNNs) and a Transformer reaches 96.02 % on UCI-HAR — [Li et al., Frontiers in Neuroscience 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2023.1233037)

Evidence for context (more than one segment helps)
- Sleep, single epoch versus sequence on MASS: "Averaged over all experimented sequence lengths, an accuracy gain of 3.4 % absolute is obtained by SeqSleepNet over the E2E-ARNN baseline", and 5.6 % for the end-to-end DeepSleepNet over its bare CNN; MF1 78.4 → 83.3 — [Phan et al., SeqSleepNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10932)
- Sleep, SNN ablations on Sleep-EDF-78 (Table 1): full model 74.2 % accuracy / 70.5 MF1; without the cross-epoch leaky state module 70.2 / 67.8; baseline without any cross-epoch context 64.8 / 62.7 — [NeuroSleep, arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15888)
- Wrist activity: adding a hidden Markov model over the window sequence raises MF1 / κ from .716 / .704 to .800 / .814 for the CNN and from .711 / .665 to .789 / .772 for the random forest (activity levels); for daily-living activities CNN .485 → .576. The RNN that sees up to 8 windows (80 s) scores .776 / .790 and still gains from the HMM (.787 / .800), "suggesting that longer sequence modelling would be fruitful" — [CAPTURE-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19229)
- Neuron memory in short-window activity recognition (Table 4, membrane decay factor set to 0 versus best value): UCI-HAR spiking CNN 95.48 vs 96.40, spiking DeepConvLSTM 94.36 vs 98.86; SHAR 93.54 vs 94.04 and 89.53 vs 92.08 — [Li et al., Frontiers in Neuroscience 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2023.1233037)
- ECG wave labelling: with identical size (1.8 K parameters), a LIF recurrent network reaches 77.8 ± 1.85 % and an adaptive-neuron network 86.88 ± 0.40 % — [Baronig et al., Nature Communications 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07517)
- Beat-level arrhythmia with neighbours: a premature atrial beat "is an abnormal beat only because it appears too soon and disrupts the rhythm … has the same shape as a normal beat, so taken alone, you can nearly not make the difference … The model will need to construct features about the nearby beats" — [Icentia11k paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09570)
- Raw audio at 16 kHz: models without a long-range mechanism collapse on the 16,000-step input (Performer 30.77, NRDE 16.49, ExpRNN 11.6; Transformer not runnable) while the same Transformer reaches 90.75 on 161-step MFCC input — [S4, ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)
- EEG decoding of which movie is being watched (4 classes, Healthy Brain Network data): S5 accuracy 55.3 % at 8-s segments, 61.3 % at 16 s, 94.4 ± 0.7 % at 32 s, 98.7 ± 0.6 % at 64 s, 95.8 ± 0.6 % at 128 s; the split is "subject-mixed" (Table 1) — [Ergezer, arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21215)

### Inferences
- EigenWorms and SCP1: ROCKET (86.3, 86.6) and the interval forest CIF (90.3, 85.9) sit inside the range of the sequence models under the Log-NCDE protocol (EigenWorms 70.9–95.0, SCP1 79.8–89.9). The splits differ (30 resamples of the default split versus 70/15/15), so the comparison is indicative only, but it gives no sign that modelling the order of distant segments is needed. This agrees with the earlier notes' finding that EigenWorms survives heavy time compression.
- Three of the six "long" UEA sets (Heartbeat, MotorImagery, SCP2) are at the majority-class rate for every method in both protocols, and a fourth (EthanolConcentration) is not a time series; deep sequence models there score 21–34 % where a shapelet method scores 82 %. They should not be used to argue anything about order or memory.
- Sleep staging is the candidate with the best-measured context effect, but the effect is "about ten neighbouring epochs help", not "order helps": no source shuffles the neighbouring epochs. Sleep-stage transition rules are directional in physiology, so an order test (shuffle the context epochs, keep the target) is cheap to run and would be new; its outcome is not predictable from the literature read here.
- In CAPTURE-24 a random forest plus an HMM (0.789) beats the 8-window RNN without one (0.776): most of the gain is smoothing over label persistence. A two-path model would have to beat "per-window classifier + HMM", not just the per-window classifier.
- The 16 kHz Speech Commands result shows that an attention model fails for length reasons while recurrent / state-space accumulation works, which matches the lab's architecture story; but there is no evidence there that order of distant segments matters beyond what a deep convolutional network with pooling captures (ConvNet 95.51 on SC35).
- The QTDB result (LIF 77.8 vs adaptive neuron 86.9) shows that neuron-state dynamics matter for labelling the phase within a heartbeat. The horizon is one beat inside a 1.3-s segment, so it demonstrates order sensitivity but not accumulation over a long past.
- For beat-level MIT-BIH the published reasoning (premature beats are defined by timing relative to neighbours) says context of a few beats is necessary for the supraventricular class; that is a short, order-dependent context — closer to what the lab's sequential path provides than single-beat classification, on which the lab found no order benefit.

### Gaps
- No shuffled-segment, bag-of-segments or "attention without positional encoding" control was found for Sleep-EDF, PTB-XL, CPSC 2018, CinC 2017, CHB-MIT, CAPTURE-24 or raw Speech Commands. One search for shuffled-epoch sleep ablations returned none.
- The gain from neighbouring-beat or RR-interval context on MIT-BIH under the inter-patient split was seen only in a search summary (DenseNet + GRU: supraventricular F1 51.08 → 61.25, [arXiv 1810.04121](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04121)); unverified, and it is a whole-model comparison rather than an ablation.
- No context-length ablation for seizure detection (CHB-MIT, TUH) was read.
- The UCR permutation study covers the univariate archive only; no equivalent permutation test on the UEA multivariate sets was found, and the paper's venue beyond arXiv was not verified.
- The EEG movie-decoding result uses a subject-mixed split and a single-author preprint; it is weak evidence.

## Q5. Practical points: size, access, preprocessing, training cost

### Takeaway
The cheapest candidates to stand up are those the lab's pipeline already covers (CinC 2017, MIT-BIH with neighbouring beats) and the small fixed-protocol sets (QTDB wave labelling with 1.8 K-parameter models, UEA sets with a few hundred series); CAPTURE-24 and raw Speech Commands are open but large (0.6 M windows × 1,000 samples; 24 K clips × 16,000 samples), and Icentia11k (271 GB) is impractical.

### Cited Findings
- CinC 2017: 8,528 public records, 9–61 s at 300 Hz; the 3,658-record test set is hidden, so published post-challenge numbers use the organisers' server or self-made cross-validation — [Clifford et al.](https://www.physionet.org/files/challenge-2017/1.0.0/papers/065-469.pdf)
- UEA sets: 259 EigenWorms series in the archive (128 + 131), 23 duplicates to remove; the Log-NCDE protocol needs five re-splits and a grid search; run times and memory are tabulated per model (EigenWorms GPU memory 2.5–13.5 GB across models in the LinOSS table) — [Log-NCDE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18512); [LinOSS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03943)
- CAPTURE-24: released under CC BY 4.0 at the Oxford research archive; 618,129 training-side windows of 3 × 1,000 samples; deep baselines used 80 / 20 participants for training / validation and batch size 512 — [CAPTURE-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19229)
- PTB-XL: 21,837 records × 10 s × 12 leads; the benchmark runs at 100 Hz and finds no compelling gain from 500 Hz; fixed folds 1–8 / 9 / 10 — [Strodthoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13701)
- Icentia11k: 271.27 GB — [Tan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09570)
- QTDB wave labelling: 105 recordings; the SNN papers reuse the preprocessed level-crossing files of Yin et al.; the QT database itself is public on PhysioNet — [Baronig et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07517); [Higuchi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14603)
- Speech Commands (35 classes): 24,482 training clips of 16,000 samples; S4/S5-class models with 0.2–0.3 M parameters reach 96–97 % — [S5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04933)
- Sleep-EDF SNN setups: band-pass 0.5–35 Hz, resampling to 100 Hz, single Fpz-Cz channel, subject-wise 5-fold with 15 % of training subjects held out for validation — [NeuroSleep](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15888)
- CPSC 2021: two leads at 200 Hz, variable-length records, AF episodes of at least five beats, stage-1 / stage-2 training sets of 730 / 706 records, test set never released — [PhysioNet CPSC 2021](https://physionet.org/content/cpsc2021/) (page content read via search summary and the earlier notes)

### Inferences
- Token budgets with the lab's patching (my arithmetic): raw Speech Commands at 64-sample patches gives 250 tokens per clip and 24,482 clips per epoch, the same order as CinC 2017 (117 tokens × 8,528 records) times about six; CAPTURE-24 at the same patch size gives about 16 tokens per window but 0.6 M windows, so sequence-of-windows training is the expensive part; a Sleep-EDF epoch (3,000 samples at 100 Hz) gives about 47 tokens, and a 15-epoch context about 700 tokens.
- Data sets with hidden test sets (CinC 2017, CPSC 2018, CPSC 2021) force self-made cross-validation, which is exactly where cross-paper comparisons break; PTB-XL, CAPTURE-24 and Speech Commands have fixed public splits and are safer for claims against published numbers.
- Sleep-EDF's many protocol variants (20 vs 78 subjects, 20-fold vs 10-fold vs 5-fold, in-bed trimming) mean a result is comparable only to papers using the identical variant; the two SNN baselines differ from each other.

### Gaps
- Licence and access terms were not re-read this session for Sleep-EDF, SHHS, ISRUC, TUH, CHB-MIT, PTB-XL and Chapman. From general knowledge (unverified here): Sleep-EDF, CHB-MIT, PTB-XL and QTDB are open-access PhysioNet sets without credentialing; SHHS requires a data-use agreement with the National Sleep Research Resource; TUH requires registration.
- Published wall-clock training times for small models on these sets were not collected, except what the earlier notes record for the lab's own CinC runs.

## Q6. Which 3–5 candidates best satisfy all three conditions (order + accumulation matter, reliable baselines incl. SNN, small model near published level)?

### Takeaway
No candidate passes all three conditions on published evidence; in particular none has a published order control. The best compromises are (1) sleep staging on Sleep-EDF with a sequence of epochs, (2) raw Speech Commands SC10 / SC35, (3) QTDB ECG wave labelling as the SNN-comparable sanity task, (4) CAPTURE-24 window sequences, and (5) keeping CinC 2017 but with the challenge metric and cross-validation baselines; the UEA long sets, 12-lead rhythm sets and CHB-MIT are poor fits.

### Cited Findings
- Sleep-EDF: context gain measured (single epoch → sequence +3.4 to +5.6 accuracy points on MASS; SNN ablation −4.0 points without cross-epoch state), SNN baselines exist (PicoSleepNet MF1 75.2 / 68.1; NeuroSleep MF1 70.5), DNN level MF1 ≈ 0.79–0.80, saturation at 10–15 epochs — [SeqSleepNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10932); [NeuroSleep](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15888); [PicoSleepNet](https://doi.org/10.1109/jbhi.2025.3602502); [Wang & Strodthoff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17779)
- Raw Speech Commands: fixed split, 16,000-step input, small-model state of the art (S5, 280 K parameters, 96.52 on SC35; S4 98.32 on SC10), ICLR-level SNN baseline (P-SpikeSSM 95.6 on SC10, 96.23 on SC35), attention baselines fail at this length — [S5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04933); [S4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396); [P-SpikeSSM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02923)
- QTDB wave labelling: per-step labels whose answer depends on position within the beat; SNN baselines from Nature Machine Intelligence, ICML and Nature Communications between 85.9 and 87.0 with about 1.8 K parameters; LIF baseline 77.8 — [Yin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11633); [Higuchi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14603); [Baronig et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07517)
- CAPTURE-24: fixed participant split, open licence, largest measured sequence effect (CNN κ .704 → .814 with an HMM over windows), baselines are simple models — [CAPTURE-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19229)
- CinC 2017: like-for-like single-model baselines are 74.6–79.2 (3-class F1, 5-fold cross-validation), hidden-test winners 0.83 — [Zihlmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06122); [Clifford et al.](https://www.physionet.org/files/challenge-2017/1.0.0/papers/065-469.pdf)
- Against the UEA long sets: position-pooled ROCKET 86.28 and interval forest 90.33 on EigenWorms; four of six sets uninformative — [Ruiz et al.](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10618-020-00727-3.pdf)
- Against 12-lead rhythm sets: performance peaks at 2–3 s of input on PTB-XL; Chapman rhythm above 0.98 macro-AUC for all models — [Mehari & Strodthoff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07579)

### Inferences

| Rank | Candidate | Order + accumulation matter | Reliable baselines (SNN?) | Small model near published level | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- Not recommended: EigenWorms / SCP1 (order not needed, tiny, noisy; usable only as a "standard table" entry because an SNN baseline exists); EthanolConcentration, Heartbeat, MotorImagery, SCP2 (uninformative); PTB-XL rhythm, CPSC 2018, Chapman (10-s records, 2–3 s suffices, no SNN baselines); CHB-MIT (no shared protocol, random-segment splits); UCI-HAR / SHAR / HHAR (saturated, short windows); Icentia11k (271 GB, no supervised standard).
- Two candidates outside the list deserve a cheap check because the pipeline already exists: (a) MIT-BIH with neighbouring-beat / RR context under the inter-patient split, scored on the supraventricular class, where physiology says a few beats of ordered context are required; (b) CPSC 2021, where the record-level class (persistent vs paroxysmal vs no AF) and episode boundaries need the whole record (earlier notes). Both lack verified baseline numbers in this session.
- Because no published order control exists for any candidate, the lab's own diagnostic (retrain with shuffled segment order; evaluate an order-blind model) has to be run on the chosen set before any model comparison; on sleep staging and CAPTURE-24 the relevant control is shuffling the neighbouring epochs / windows while keeping the target in place, and adding a smoothing baseline (HMM or majority filter over per-segment predictions).

### Gaps
- Whether order (rather than context) matters on Sleep-EDF, CAPTURE-24 and raw Speech Commands is unknown from the literature read; this is the decisive missing evidence for the ranking.
- SNN baselines for CAPTURE-24, PTB-XL, CPSC 2018 / 2021 were not found; absence is from OpenAlex title/abstract searches and one web search each, not a proof of non-existence.
- Like-for-like small-model numbers (0.2–1.5 M parameters) on Sleep-EDF under the PicoSleepNet protocol were not verified in full text (PicoSleepNet is abstract-only here).
- SHHS, ISRUC, TUH, WISDM, PAMAP2 and Opportunity were not evaluated against the three conditions with primary sources.
