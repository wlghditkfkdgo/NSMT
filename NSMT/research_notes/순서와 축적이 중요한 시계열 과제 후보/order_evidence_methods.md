# How papers establish that a sequence task requires temporal ORDER and long-range ACCUMULATION: diagnostics, datasets with published evidence, and what reviewers accept (state as of November 2026)

Reading conventions used in these notes

- "Read directly" = I downloaded the paper (arXiv PDF or PMC full text) and read the table / section named. Numbers are copied from those tables.
- "Review paraphrase" = content of public OpenReview reviews obtained through the OpenReview search API and summarised by an automated reader. - Venue labels were checked either in the paper header or in OpenAlex; where neither was possible the item is marked "venue not re-verified" or "arXiv-only".

- Terms. "Per-sample shuffle" = a new random order for every sample. "Fixed permutation" = one random order applied identically to every training and test sample (psMNIST style). "Test-time" = model trained on intact data, perturbed only at evaluation. "Retrain" = model trained and tested on the perturbed data. "Memory-less control" = the same network with state carried across time steps switched off.

## 1. Diagnostics in the literature: which papers introduced or prominently used each, and what pitfalls are documented

### Takeaway
The accepted way to show that a TASK needs order is to retrain on order-destroyed input (or to give an order-blind model the same information) and show a loss; perturbing only at test time shows what a trained MODEL uses and is explicitly criticised as confounding information loss with distribution shift. Three properties are routinely conflated and need separate controls: long context (truncation), order (per-sample shuffle with retraining, time reversal), and accumulation in state (memory-less control; note that parity-type tasks need accumulation but not order).

### Cited Findings

Shuffle / permutation controls: test-time versus retrain

- The retrain requirement was formalised for feature-removal tests by ROAR (NeurIPS 2019): "Without retraining, it is unclear whether degradation in performance is due to the introduction of artifacts outside of the original training distribution or because we actually removed information" (Sec. 3); in their ImageNet experiment a random modification of 90 % of inputs dropped a non-retrained model to 0.5 % accuracy, far more than with retraining (Sec. 4.3.1 / Fig. 3 text). — [Hooker et al., ROAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10758)
- O'Connor & Andreas separate "usable information" (ablate the context at train AND eval, V-information) from "test-time sensitivity" (the Khandelwal et al. paradigm, ablate at eval only), stating that the two paradigms "characterize different aspects of model behavior" and that V-information "more clearly distinguished between prediction errors attributable to loss of information and prediction errors attributable to malformed and out-of-distribution model inputs" (Sec. 3.3). With train+eval ablation, shuffling word order within sentences and deleting all words other than nouns removed less than 15 % of usable information in mid- and long-range context (abstract). — [O'Connor & Andreas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08367) (venue: ACL 2021 by my memory; OpenAlex lists only the arXiv/DSpace copies)
- Test-time shuffle in NLP: 75–90 % of correct predictions of BERT-based GLUE classifiers stay constant after input words are randomly shuffled (abstract). — [Pham et al., Findings of ACL 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15180)
- Retrain in NLP: models trained on sentences with randomly permuted word order "perform close to state-of-the-art models" on GLUE tasks (abstract); RoBERTa pre-trained on shuffled sentences still reaches high downstream accuracy, and the authors conclude evaluation datasets need to be curated that require order (abstract). — [Gupta et al., AAAI 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03453); [Sinha et al., EMNLP 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06644)
- Documented pitfall, leakage of order through the shuffling procedure: Abdou et al. show shuffled-text language models "retain information pertaining to the original, naturalistic word order", partly because earlier work shuffled before rather than after sub-word segmentation, and partly through statistical dependencies between sentence length and unigram probabilities; they also show that beyond GLUE "a variety of language understanding tasks do require word order information" (abstract). — [Abdou et al., ACL 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10995)
- Retrain in video, the cleanest precedent for choosing a benchmark by an order test: TRN was trained with ordered versus shuffled frames; "the significant difference on the Something-Something dataset shows the importance of the temporal order", whereas on UCF101 the authors "observe no difference between the ordered frames and shuffled frames", concluding that UCF101-type recognition "doesn't necessarily require the temporal reasoning ability" (Sec. "Importance of temporal order", Fig. 6a). — [Zhou et al., TRN, ECCV 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08496)
- Human (not model) shuffle test to define a "Temporal Dataset" of classes that cannot be recognised when frames are shuffled; the authors chose humans because selecting temporal classes with a model "would bias the process" (abstract, Sec. 1–3). — [Sevilla-Lara et al., WACV 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08340)
- Forecasting, test-time-style shuffle: Zeng et al. shuffle the raw look-back window ("Shuf.") or swap its halves ("Half-Ex."). Table 5: on ETTh1 the average MSE increase is 81.06 % / 4.78 % for Linear, 73.28 % / 3.44 % for FEDformer, 56.91 % / 0.46 % for Autoformer, 1.98 % / 0.18 % for Informer; on Exchange it is 27.26 % / 46.81 % for Linear and within about ±1.2 % for the three Transformers. The text does not state whether models were retrained on shuffled input. — [Zeng et al., AAAI 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13504)
- The same three perturbations (shuffle all, shuffle first half, exchange halves) plus masking were reused by Tan et al. (NeurIPS 2024). Table 6 (ETTh1, horizon 96): shuffle-all degrades Time-LLM by 51.8 % and its no-LLM ablation by 56.0 %; shuffling only the first half degrades them by 5.6 % / 4.5 %. The authors use the comparison to argue LLM-based forecasters are "no more vulnerable to input shuffling than their ablations" (Sec. 4.4). — [Tan et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)
- Review paraphrase (ICLR 2026 submission, withdrawn): a paper proposing a "Temporal Order Sensitivity Test (TOST)" that permutes time steps consistently at training and test was credited by two reviewers for avoiding the "permute-only-at-inference" pitfall; one reviewer (rating 4) added that on datasets with regime shifts or calendar effects a fixed permutation may change the distribution in non-temporal ways and asked for several independent permutations. — [OpenReview forum 8vgP9VhECt](https://openreview.net/forum?id=8vgP9VhECt)

Fixed permutation is a different test from per-sample shuffling

- psMNIST was introduced with "a fixed random permutation of the pixels" to make pixel-by-pixel MNIST harder for recurrent nets (Sec. 4.2). — [Le et al., IRNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.00941) (arXiv-only)
- Zhang et al.'s "Temporal Information Removal Test" for UCR applies "the same permutation to every instance" in train and test; the transformation "is invariant for tabular methods or Euclidean distance" and removes sub-sequence (local shape) features (Sec. 4). — [Zhang, Batista & Kanhere 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20264) (LNCS conference paper per OpenAlex; conference name not re-verified)
- Swelam et al. describe that test as "destroying local order while leaving each value at a fixed position" (Sec. 4). — [Swelam et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09843) (NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper per its footer; arXiv)

Bag-of-features / order-free baselines

- BagNet: a ResNet-50 variant that classifies from occurrences of small local patches "without taking into account their spatial ordering" reaches 87.6 % top-5 on ImageNet with 33 × 33 px features (abstract). — [Brendel & Bethge, ICLR 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00760)
- Time series, distribution-only baseline: a linear classifier on two features that "ignore the sequential ordering of the data" (mean and standard deviation) beat chance on 69 of 128 UCR/UEA univariate problems (53.9 %) and reached 100 % on InsectEPGRegularTrain and GunPointOldVersusYoung (abstract, Sec. 3). — [Henderson, Bryant & Fulcher 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17809) (arXiv-only)
- Time series, tabular baseline: tabular models with no time ordering outperform the ROCKET family on about 19 % of univariate and 28 % of multivariate UCR/UEA datasets and are within 10 percentage points on about 50 % (abstract). — [Dhariyal, Le Nguyen & Ifrim 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07886) (LNCS workshop paper per OpenAlex)
- Spiking datasets: the SHD/SSC dataset paper itself validated the dataset with a count-only control: SVMs trained on spike-count vectors with all temporal information removed were far below LSTMs/CNNs given temporal histograms (Fig. 3, "Temporal information is essential to classify"); on SSC the count-only classifiers did not get past the 30 % mark. — [Cramer et al., IEEE TNNLS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07407)
- Counter-example in neuromorphic vision: an ANN on time-collapsed frames reached 99.23 % on N-MNIST and 78.01 % on N-Caltech101, and the authors conclude "precise spike timings are not important in N-MNIST" (abstract). — [Iyer, Chua & Li, Front. Neurosci. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01013)

Removing positional encoding from Transformers

- A Transformer block without positional information is permutation equivariant (Claim 1). — [Yun et al., ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10077)
- Pitfall: removing PE does not make every Transformer order-blind. Causal language models without any PE "acquire an implicit notion of absolute positions", conjectured to come from the causal mask (abstract). — [Haviv et al., Findings of EMNLP 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16634). Decoder-only Transformers without PE (NoPE) outperformed explicit PEs on length generalisation and can represent absolute and relative positions (abstract). — [Kazemnejad et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19466)
- Pitfall, position leaking through the output head in patch-based forecasters: zeroing the positional encodings of a trained PatchTST left MSE unchanged (0.142 → 0.142), while a ViT fell from 89.8 % to 32.4 % (Table 2). The authors' explanation: PatchTST "adds a flatten layer and a linear layer after the transformer blocks, where the relative positions of the linear layer weights corresponding to different tokens are fixed. As a result, the model is able to recognize the relative positions of the tokens even in the absence of positional encoding" (Sec. 3.4). — [Liang, Zhu & Sun 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20942) (arXiv-only, "under review")
- Pitfall, position leaking through convolution padding: abstract states that a CNN filter "may know what it is looking at, but not where it is positioned" and the paper studies how absolute position is nevertheless encoded. — [Islam, Jia & Bruce, ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08248) (only the abstract was read)
- PE ablation in forecasting: Table 6 (Traffic, look-back 96). Removing positional embeddings barely changes FEDformer (0.597 → 0.587 at horizon 96) and Autoformer (0.629 → 0.613) but hurts Informer (0.719 → 1.035); the authors attribute this to FEDformer/Autoformer embedding a sequence of timestamps per token. — [Zeng et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13504)

Context truncation / look-back and input-position controls

- "Close" versus "Far" input: forecasting the same 720 future steps from the 96 steps just before them or from 96 steps that end 96 steps earlier changes FEDformer MSE from 0.251 to 0.265 on Electricity and 0.631 to 0.645 on Traffic (Table 3); the authors use this to support their hypothesis that long-term forecasting depends mainly on capturing trend and periodicity. — [Zeng et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13504)
- Last-k versus random-k subset on a classification benchmark: UnICORNN on EigenWorms needs "at least the last 10k entries" to reach 80 % when trained on the last entries, and "using a random subset of the entries increases the test accuracy significantly when compared to using only the last entries", which the authors read as the information being "uniformly distributed throughout the full sequence" (Sec. 4, Fig. 3). — [Rusch & Mishra, UnICORNN, ICML 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05487). This is the evidence later cited as EigenWorms having dependencies "longer than 10k". — [Rusch et al., LEM, ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04744)

Memory-less / reset-state ablations of stateful models (the spiking-specific diagnostic)

- Segregated Temporal Probe (STP): three training modes for the same SNN. STBP keeps all temporal pathways; SDBP "stops gradient propagation along the temporal domain during the backward pass"; NoTD "stops the propagation of information along the temporal domain during both passes, effectively treating each time step independently" (Sec. I–II). Criteria: if NoTD ≈ STBP the dataset does not need temporal processing; if SDBP ≈ STBP it does not need temporal credit assignment. — [Ma et al., "SNNs for Temporal Processing: Status Quo and Future Prospects"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09449) (IEEE TNNLS 2026 per OpenAlex; I read arXiv v1)
- The same probe is built into the Neuromorphic Sequential Arena benchmark and used to validate its tasks before comparing neuron models (Sec. 2.3, Table 2). — [Chen et al., NSA, IJCAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22035)
- Reset ablation as a memory argument: PSN reports column-sequential CIFAR10 accuracy 79.50 % for "LIF wo reset" versus 81.50 % LIF and 88.45 % PSN (Table 1). — [Fang et al., PSN, NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12760)

Order-specific perturbations other than shuffling

- Time reversal of spike trains "perturb[s] spatio-temporal spike patterns but leave[s] interspike intervals and coincidence information unchanged"; SNNs without delays "perform well under time reversal, whereas networks trained with delays perform poorly" (abstract, Sec. 4.4). The paper trains new models from scratch for jitter and deletion but reuses pretrained networks for time reversal (Sec. 4.1), i.e. reversal is used as a test-time probe of what the model uses. — [Yu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16043) (Neuromorphic Computing and Engineering 2026 per OpenAlex)
- For attribution evaluation on time series, Schlegel et al. introduce "two sequence verification" perturbations in addition to point perturbation (Sec. 1, 3). — [Schlegel et al., ICCV Workshops 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.07082) (only the introduction lines were read; details of the swap/mean perturbations are from my memory and not re-verified)

Gradient / attribution over time

- Saliency methods across RNN, TCN and Transformer "fail to reliably and accurately identify feature importance over time in time series data", mainly because of "the conflation of time and feature domains" (abstract). — [Ismail et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13924)
- The effective receptive field "only occupies a fraction of the full theoretical receptive field" (abstract), the standard caution against equating architectural reach with used context. — [Luo et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04128)
- In SNN papers, gradient-over-time plots are used as supporting evidence only: TC-LIF visualises error gradients across time on S-MNIST (Fig. 3); the dual-memory-pathway paper uses last-time-step supervision and reports that first-layer gradients "remained non-negligible over the full 784 steps of S-MNIST and over 200 steps of PS-MNIST" (Sec. 2.3). — [Zhang et al., TC-LIF, AAAI 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13250); [Sun et al., Nat. Mach. Intell. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07602)

Synthetic memory-length tasks and statistical "long memory"

- Adding problem and copy memory are the standard synthetic probes; TCN evaluates adding (T = 600) and copy memory (T = 1000) (Table 1) and credits the adding problem to Hochreiter & Schmidhuber (1997). — [Bai, Kolter & Koltun 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01271) (arXiv-only)
- Mamba introduces Selective Copying (a modification of the Copying task of Arjovsky et al. 2016 with random spacing) and uses Induction Heads; time-invariant linear recurrences and global convolutions solve Copying but not Selective Copying (Sec. 3.1, Fig. 2). — [Gu & Dao, Mamba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752) (arXiv)
- xLSTM calls its Multi-Query Associative Recall experiment a test of "memory capacities" (Sec. 4.1, Fig. 5). — [Beck et al., xLSTM, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04517)
- Statistical definition: "long memory happens to be a well-defined and long-existing terminology in statistics"; RNN and LSTM with Markovian updates are proved not to have long memory in that sense (abstract, Sec. 1). — [Zhao et al., ICML 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03860). Current practice measures long-range dependence "heuristically against some task or tasks in which success is taken an indicator of 'memory' in a colloquial sense" (Sec. 1). — [Greaves-Tunnell & Harchaoui, ICML 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03834)

### Inferences
- The literature supports a two-by-two reading that reviewers increasingly apply: (test-time, retrain) × (model property, task property). Test-time shuffling answers "does this trained model use order"; only retraining (or an order-blind model given the same inputs) answers "does the task reward order". The lab already has both; the retrain result is the one to put in the main text, with the test-time result as a model-behaviour analysis.
- A fixed permutation and a per-sample shuffle test different things. A fixed permutation keeps position identity and is learnable by any model with positional parameters or a dense layer (psMNIST is exactly this); it tests reliance on local shape / the convolutional prior. Only a per-sample shuffle removes order information. Papers that say "temporal order is irrelevant" on the basis of a fixed permutation (Zhang et al. 2025, as summarised by Swelam et al. 2026) are making the weaker statement.
- Order and accumulation are separable. In Delétang et al.'s table (see Section 3), Parity Check and Cycle Navigation are marked permutation-invariant yet Transformers fail and RNN/LSTM succeed: these tasks need a running state but not order. A "sequential memory" claim therefore needs one control per property: per-sample shuffle retraining (order), memory-less / NoTD (accumulation in state), truncation (context length).
- "Order-blind without PE" has to be shown, not assumed. Documented leaks are the causal mask, a flatten-then-linear head, convolution padding, and, in a spiking model with one token per time step, the neuron state itself. The lab's own observation that ETT order sensitivity sits in the flat head matches the PatchTST zero-PE result above.
- Truncation curves alone are weak evidence for memory. UnICORNN's own Fig. 3 shows a random subset of EigenWorms entries beating the same number of last entries, which is what coverage (evidence accumulation), not ordered long-range dependency, would produce.

### Gaps
- I did not find any peer-reviewed paper that runs per-sample shuffle WITH retraining systematically across time-series classification or forecasting benchmarks. The closest items are the fixed-permutation UCR study and one withdrawn ICLR 2026 submission (TOST).
- Whether the Zeng et al. and Tan et al. shuffle experiments retrained the models is not stated in the text I read; both read as evaluation-time perturbations. The released code was not inspected.
- Reservoir "memory capacity" (Jaeger 2001), "information processing capacity" (Dambre et al. 2012) and the LMU capacity task (Voelker et al. 2019) were not opened in this session; no claims about them are made here.
- Schlegel et al.'s exact definition of the sequence perturbations and Islam et al.'s experiments were not read beyond the abstract/introduction.

## 2. Datasets for which order / long-range evidence has been published

### Takeaway
Published dataset-level evidence exists for (a) the UCR archive, where about one third of datasets are insensitive to a fixed permutation and half are beaten at chance level by mean/std alone; (b) spiking/neuromorphic benchmarks, where a peer-reviewed memory-less probe shows static and DVS vision sets need no temporal processing while GSC, SHD, SSC, TIMIT and the seven NSA tasks do; and (c) forecasting sets, where sensitivity is to local order only. For LRA, pixel-sequence tasks and EigenWorms the published evidence is about context length or locality, not order.

### Cited Findings

UCR / UEA time-series classification

- Fixed-permutation test on the 112 equal-length UCR datasets, five runs each: accuracy on permuted data was higher than, or within the sum of standard deviations of, the original for 38 datasets (34 %) with MiniRocket and 36 (32 %) with catch22; 12 datasets for each classifier had permuted accuracy equal or higher; with two sums of standard deviations the counts rise to 55 (49 %) and 56 (50 %). Rotation Forest (tabular) versus HIVE-COTE 2 under the same rule: 44 datasets within one and 60 within two standard deviations (Sec. 4). — [Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20264)
- Causes named: "perfect alignment" (eight spectroscopy datasets such as Strawberry, where every index is the same wavelength) and "overly well-segmented" data (e.g. UMD) (Sec. 5). The authors' remedy, "UCR Augmented", pads both ends with random-walk segments of random length to break alignment; under it Rotation Forest and the phase-dependent interval method CIF degrade most, shapelet (STC), MiniRocket and WEASEL least (Sec. 6–7, Fig. 3). — [Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20264)
- Provenance audit of all 128 UCR datasets: for 52 the classification target "is not a real-world prediction problem"; 49 are "ordered, non-temporal" (wavelength, contour), 71 are sampled over time, 8 synthetic (Fig. 1, Sec. 3.1). Because every series is pre-extracted and often aligned, "time steps become features" (Sec. 4). — [Swelam et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09843)
- Order-free statistics already separate many UCR problems: mean + standard deviation beats chance on 69/128. — [Henderson et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17809)
- Algorithm taxonomy that encodes what kind of information each family uses: interval-based methods use "phase dependent intervals"; shapelet-based use "phase independent discriminatory subseries"; dictionary-based use "histograms of counts of repeating patterns"; BOSS "intentionally ignores the locations of words in series, classifying based on the frequency of patterns rather than their location" (Sec. 3, 4.5.5). The best-ranked families (ROCKET, HIVE-COTE) "combine convolution/shapelet approaches with dictionary based ones, i.e. they look for the presence of or the frequency of subseries" (concluding section). — [Middlehurst, Schäfer & Bagnall, "Bake off redux", DMKD 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13029)
- UEA multivariate, effect of removing position information from a Transformer: in a single-layer Transformer run five times on all 30 UEA datasets, the model without position encoding has the worst average rank (6.03 versus 2.36 for the best combination, Fig. 6) and "the least accurate results" (ablation section). No per-dataset no-PE table is given. — [Foumani et al., ConvTran, DMKD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16642)
- A 2025–2026 survey benchmark reports per-dataset No-PE accuracy for two Transformer backbones, e.g. Sleep 83.2 ± 2.1 (No PE) versus 88.8 ± 0.5 (best), EpilepticSeizure ("ED") 65.8 versus 75.5, and JapaneseVowels (29 steps) 93.5 versus 99.2 (Sec. 5.1, Table 3). The running text is internally inconsistent about the JapaneseVowels gap (listed under "small gaps (2–4 %)" and elsewhere as 5.5–5.7 %), so treat individual numbers with caution. — [Irani & Metsis, PE survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12370) (Int. J. Mach. Learn. Cybern. 2026 per OpenAlex)

Spiking / neuromorphic benchmarks (memory-less probe, Table I of Ma et al.; the audio rows are repeated identically in NSA Table S4)

- No temporal processing needed (NoTD ≈ STBP): MNIST 99.40 → 99.18, CIFAR10 94.86 → 93.46, CIFAR100 74.57 → 73.28 (static images repeated over time); N-MNIST 99.49 → 99.09; CIFAR10-DVS 78.50 → 80.00 (NoTD higher); DvsGesture 95.14 → 94.44. — [Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09449)
- Forward temporal state needed, temporal credit assignment hardly needed: GSC STBP 92.91 / SDBP 89.00 / NoTD 77.53; SHD 86.48 / 85.07 / 68.51; SSC 67.13 / 66.03 / 44.97; TIMIT 57.07 / 53.24 / 49.01. The authors conclude these audio sets "are not effective for" testing temporal credit assignment, and that "a significant subset of samples can be accurately classified using only frame-level processing" (Sec. III). — [Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09449)
- Benchmarks the same authors validate as temporal (STBP > SDBP > NoTD on all three, Fig. 6): Penn Treebank next-word prediction (70-token sequences), PS-MNIST (784 steps, fixed permutation), and a binary adding task with adjustable length (Sec. IV). They also report that SOTA SNNs "still lag behind ANN models in their ability to model long-range temporal dependencies" (Sec. I). — [Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09449)
- NSA (IJCAI 2025) tasks with their probe results (Table 2; STBP / SDBP / NoTD): AL, a synthetic robot-localisation task with user-defined length, 63.52 / 58.52 / 53.34; HAR on WISDM gyroscope, length 200, 18 classes, 81.27 / 76.29 / 67.75; EEG motor imagery on OpenBMI, length 500, 65.20 / 57.48 / 52.48; sound-source localisation on SLoClas, length 500, 8.88 / 5.79 / 3.45 (metric column printed as accuracy; I could not confirm the unit); automatic lip-reading on DVS-Lip (event camera), length 200, 17.83 / 2.47 / 2.01; audio denoising SI-SNR 11.47 / 10.18 / 9.47 dB; ASR CER 20.70 / 26.30 / 27.10 %. Backbones are MLP SNNs (Table 1). — [Chen et al., NSA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22035)

Forecasting benchmarks

- ETTh1: full shuffle of the look-back hurts order-aware models strongly (Linear +81.06 %), swapping halves hardly (+4.78 %); Exchange: Linear +27.26 % / +46.81 %, Transformers about 0 (Table 5). — [Zeng et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13504)
- ETTh1 (horizon 96) and Illness (horizon 24) with LLM-based and ablated models: shuffle-all 50–69 % and 69–118 % degradation respectively; shuffling only the first half 3–12 % on ETTh1 (Table 6). — [Tan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)
- PatchTST and iTransformer on ECL, Traffic, Weather, ETTh1/h2/m1/m2: replacing learned embeddings by fixed ones or zeroing PE changes MSE in the third decimal at most (Tables 23–24, Table 2). — [Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20942) (arXiv-only)
- Benchmark practice in long-term forecasting is itself disputed: a TMLR (01/2026) study re-evaluates top supervised models and reports concerns about "inconsistent benchmarking and reporting practices" (abstract). — [Brigato et al., TMLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14045)

LRA, pixel sequences, EigenWorms, raw speech

- LRA was built with the stated goal of probing long-range dependencies (desiderata list, Sec. 2); in the original results no model learned Path-X (Table 1 note). — [Tay et al., LRA, ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04006)
- Critique from long-document NLP: after controlled pretraining, results on the LRA text tasks "often do not translate" to real long-context tasks, and attention variants perform similarly on LRA; the paper lists "pitfalls of a widely-used long-range benchmark" (abstract, Sec. 4.1). — [Xiong et al., NAACL 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07210)
- S4 was the first to solve Path-X (88 % versus 50 % chance for prior work) and reports raw-speech SC10 classification at length 16,000 (Sec. 4.2, Tables 4–5). — [Gu et al., S4, ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)
- sMNIST/psMNIST in SNN papers show that feed-forward LIF cannot carry information: the dual-memory-pathway paper reports feed-forward SNNs at 11.3 % on PS-MNIST and recurrent SNNs at 71.0 % (Table 1 text), and that S-MNIST / PS-MNIST are "largely unaffected" when the slow memory is updated only every 10 steps while SHD/SSC degrade much sooner (Sec. 2.2, Fig. 3a). — [Sun et al., Nat. Mach. Intell. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07602)
- EigenWorms: last-k versus random-k result quoted in Section 1. — [UnICORNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05487)

ECG rhythm labels that are defined by beat order

- The 12 rhythm classes of the Stanford / iRhythm single-lead study include Bigeminy, Trigeminy and Wenckebach alongside atrial fibrillation & flutter, atrio-ventricular block, ectopic atrial rhythm, idioventricular rhythm, junctional rhythm, noise, sinus rhythm, supraventricular tachycardia and ventricular tachycardia; sequence-level AUC of the network is 0.997 for Bigeminy, 0.998 for Trigeminy and 0.978 for Wenckebach (AUC table; training set 91,232 records from 53,549 patients). — [Hannun et al., Nat. Med. 2019 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6784839/)

### Inferences
- For the lab's purpose the UCR archive splits into three kinds: (i) roughly one third where even a fixed permutation costs nothing (value-at-position problems; not usable), (ii) problems solved by order-free distribution or pattern-count features (dictionary methods, mean/std; not usable for an order claim), and (iii) the remainder, where local shape matters but nothing published shows that the ARRANGEMENT of patterns along the series matters. No UCR/UEA dataset has published per-sample-shuffle-retrain evidence, so any UCR/UEA set chosen needs the lab's own diagnostic first.
- The memory-less probe literature is the closest published match to the lab's memory-less control, and it gives a ready list with numbers. Among non-excluded, non-event-audio tasks the documented ones are GSC (NoTD −15.38 points), TIMIT (−8.06), WISDM HAR at length 200 (−13.52), OpenBMI EEG-MI at length 500 (−12.72), PS-MNIST, binary adding, and PTB. Two caveats: these probes used MLP/feed-forward or recurrent SNN backbones in which neuron state is the ONLY path across time, so they show that context is needed, not that a recurrent state beats an attention block that sees all tokens; and NoTD removes accumulation without telling apart order.
- On GSC/SHD/SSC/TIMIT the gap between full training and no-temporal-gradient training is small (1–4 points), so these sets reward a forward memory trace but hardly reward learning long temporal credit assignment; a neuron with a fixed slow trace would be expected to do nearly as well as a learned one.
- Forecasting evidence is consistently "local order near the forecast origin matters, arrangement of the far half does not" (half-exchange and first-half-shuffle numbers), which is why an order-blind attention body plus an order-aware head matches a sequential path on ETT.
- Rhythm classes such as bigeminy (alternating normal and ventricular beats), trigeminy and Wenckebach are defined clinically by the order of beat types or a progressive change, so they are natural candidates for a real task where a bag of beats is insufficient in principle. This is my reasoning from the class definitions, not a published shuffle result; MIT-BIH rhythm annotations for bigeminy/trigeminy were not re-checked in this session.

### Gaps
- No published study classifies real time-series datasets by per-sample order sensitivity with retraining (see Section 1 gaps). Searches for shuffle-retrain controls on HAR, ECG and EEG returned only augmentation papers and design arguments, no diagnostic study.
- The list of the 38 / 36 UCR datasets is not printed in the Zhang et al. paper; it would have to be taken from their code repository (https://github.com/YunruiZhang/Revisit-Time-Series-Classification-Benchmark), which I did not open.
- Bake-off redux Table 18 (ranks by problem type) was located but not transcribed.
- Whether Hannun et al.'s rhythm task degrades under beat-order shuffling has not been published as far as I could find; only the 336-record test set of that study is, to my knowledge, public (not re-verified).
- Speech Commands (raw) has no published shuffle or truncation diagnostic that I could find beyond what the earlier internal report lists.

## 3. Self-attention without positional encoding, and recurrent / state-space models versus Transformers on order-critical tasks

### Takeaway
Theory and controlled experiments agree that attention fails on state-tracking tasks (parity, modular counting, non-commutative composition) where even small recurrent nets succeed, and that recurrent state loses on retrieval-type tasks. On real time-series benchmarks, removing position information costs a Transformer a few to roughly sixteen accuracy points in classification and often nothing in patch-based forecasting.

### Cited Findings

- Self-attention "cannot model periodic finite-state languages" such as parity, nor hierarchical structure, unless layers or heads grow with input length (abstract). — [Hahn, TACL 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06755)
- Empirical Chomsky-hierarchy study (20,910 models, 15 tasks; score = accuracy averaged over unseen longer lengths, best of 10 seeds). Table 2, regular tasks: Parity Check RNN 100.0 / LSTM 100.0 / Transformer 52.0; Cycle Navigation 100.0 / 100.0 / 61.9; Modular Arithmetic (Simple) 100.0 / 100.0 / 24.2; Even Pairs 100.0 / 100.0 / 96.4. Parity Check and Cycle Navigation are marked as permutation-invariant tasks. Transformers were given five positional encodings (none, sin/cos, RoPE, ALiBi, relative) and the best is reported; they "fail to generalize, regardless of their positional encodings" on most tasks (discussion of Table 2). — [Delétang et al., ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02098)
- Transformers can represent any finite-state automaton with O(log T) depth "shortcut" solutions and often learn them, but these are brittle (abstract). — [Liu et al., ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10749)
- Linear recurrent models share the weakness unless designed otherwise: "both Transformers and LRNNs struggle to perform state-tracking"; "current architectures are unable to solve even parity, the simplest state-tracking task, which non-linear RNNs can handle effectively"; allowing negative eigenvalues fixes parity for Mamba and DeltaNet (abstract). — [Grazzi et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537); design-choice analysis in [Sarrof et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17394)
- The trade-off runs both ways: a one-layer Transformer of logarithmic width can do index lookup while an RNN needs a linear-size state; constant-size RNNs recognise bounded Dyck languages while one-layer Transformers need linear size; nearest-neighbour and string-equality favour two-layer Transformers (abstract). — [Bhattamishra et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09347)
- xLSTM argues for its memory-mixing cells with exactly this battery: formal-language tasks extending Delétang et al. (parity etc.), Multi-Query Associative Recall, then LRA (Sec. 4.1, Fig. 4–5). — [Beck et al., xLSTM, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04517)
- Real time-series, classification: No-PE Transformers rank last on UEA (ConvTran) and lose 3–17 accuracy points in the survey benchmark; both cited with numbers in Section 2. — [Foumani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16642); [Irani & Metsis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12370)
- Real time-series, forecasting: PE removal or zeroing is nearly free for FEDformer, Autoformer, PatchTST and iTransformer but not for Informer (point-wise tokens); cited with numbers in Sections 1–2. — [Zeng et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13504); [Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20942)
- Spiking Transformers: adding a spiking PE to Spikformer raises average forecasting R² from .719 (w/o PE) to .744 (CPG-PE) over Metr-la, Pems-bay, Solar, Electricity (Table 1); on text classification a random spike matrix of the same size gives 77.23 versus 77.25 without PE and 79.41 with CPG-PE (Table 2). — [Lv et al., CPG-PE, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14362)
- An early counter-current in forecasting: training Transformers on windows whose time steps need not be consecutive, "breaking the temporal order", was reported as competitive (abstract). — [Jin, Ma & Yan 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09620) (arXiv-only)

### Inferences
- The tasks where recurrence is provably or empirically required are algorithmic state-tracking tasks. No published real-signal benchmark has been shown to contain such structure; this is why sequential-memory papers pair a constructed task with real benchmarks rather than relying on real benchmarks alone.
- For a model whose two paths are an order-blind attention block and a sequential neuron path, the formal-language results predict exactly the lab's observation: when the label is a retrieval or evidence-pooling function of the tokens (ETT with an order-aware head, heartbeat classification), attention suffices; a sequential path can only show an advantage when the label is a running, order-dependent function of the token stream.
- Parity-like "permutation-invariant but stateful" tasks offer a way to show accumulation separately from order: an order-blind attention path with mean pooling fails on them in Delétang et al.'s setting even though shuffling the input would not change the label.

### Gaps
- I found no study that compares recurrent or state-space models with Transformers on a real time-series task under a control that guarantees an order requirement.
- Mamba / S4 / xLSTM long-range results on real signals were not re-read beyond S4's Path-X and SC10 statements; the earlier internal notes cover PPG-DaLiA, EigenWorms and Speech Commands numbers.

## 4. How well-regarded SNN papers argue that a neuron or module provides temporal memory or positional information, and how reviewers responded

### Takeaway
The recurring pattern is a constructed delay task whose difficulty is dialled by the delay, plus sequential image benchmarks (S-MNIST, PS-MNIST, sequential CIFAR) and SHD/SSC/GSC, plus an ablation of the new component; gradient-over-time plots are supplementary. Public reviews show reviewers asking for precisely the missing piece: a task that isolates long-term dependency when only forecasting is shown, harder sequence tasks when PS-MNIST is saturated, and a control that separates the claimed mechanism from parameter count or from a non-spiking sub-module.

### Cited Findings

How each paper carries the argument (read directly unless noted)

- DH-SNN (Nat. Commun. 2024): introduces the "delayed spiking XOR problem" to test "the memory length" (Fig. 3), a "multi-timescale spiking XOR problem" for heterogeneous timescales (Fig. 4), then SHD, SSC, S-MNIST, PS-MNIST and further benchmarks; Methods state the XOR task was "elaborate[d] ... as a simple but clear benchmark" because accuracy metrics on standard sets "are not intuitive for understanding the underlying working mechanism"; a reset-mechanism analysis is included. — [Zheng et al., DH-SNN (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10766638/)
- TC-LIF (AAAI 2024): theoretical analysis of ∂U[T]/∂U[t], gradient-evolution plot on S-MNIST (Fig. 3), accuracy on S-MNIST, PS-MNIST, GSC, SHD, SSC (Table 1). — [Zhang et al., TC-LIF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13250)
- PSN (NeurIPS 2023): Sec. 4.2 "Learning Long-term Dependencies" rests on column-by-column sequential CIFAR10/100 (32 steps): PSN 88.45 / 62.21, masked PSN 85.81 / 60.69, sliding PSN 86.70 / 62.11, GLIF 83.66 / 58.92, PLIF 83.49 / 57.55, LIF 81.50 / 55.45, LIF without reset 79.50 / 53.33 (Table 1). — [Fang et al., PSN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12760)
- TS-LIF (ICLR 2025): main text argues through frequency-response analysis and forecasting on Metr-la, Pems-bay, Solar, Electricity, plus missing-value robustness; Appendix A.11 adds the delayed spiking XOR following DH-SNN (two-layer MLP, 20 hidden neurons): accuracy at delay 10 / 20 / 30 / 40 / 50 is 0.748 / 0.504 / 0.504 / 0.500 / 0.500 for LIF, 0.994 / 0.977 / 0.791 / 0.585 / 0.585 for TS-LIF, 0.5 throughout for ReLU (Table 11). — [TS-LIF, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05108)
- CPG-PE (NeurIPS 2024 spotlight per OpenReview listing): with/without-PE comparisons on forecasting, text classification and image classification, a Random-PE control for parameter count (Table 2), and an explicit research question on image patches "that have no inherent order" (Sec. 4). — [Lv et al., CPG-PE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14362)
- SeqSNN (ICML 2024): frames the problem as "temporal alignment" between series steps and SNN steps; evidence is forecasting accuracy versus ANN counterparts (RQ1) and a comparison of delta, convolutional and repetition spike encoders (RQ2, Sec. 4.3). No order or memory control is reported in the sections I read. — [Lv et al., SeqSNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01533)
- Dual memory pathways, "DMP" (Nat. Mach. - Benchmark-level critique from inside the SNN community: commonly used neuromorphic benchmarks have "critical limitations in their ability to evaluate the temporal processing capabilities of SNNs" (abstract), with the probe numbers in Section 2. — [Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09449); [NSA, IJCAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22035)

### Inferences
- What carried acceptance in these cases was not a large margin on a standard benchmark but a clear chain: mechanism → constructed task that isolates it → standard sequential benchmarks → ablation. Where the constructed task was missing (TS-LIF), a reviewer asked for it and a small delayed-XOR table sufficed.
- Reviewers in this community treat S-MNIST / PS-MNIST / sequential CIFAR / SHD-SSC-GSC as the default "temporal datasets" and ask for them by name; they also regard PS-MNIST as saturated. A paper that omits them will be asked for them even if its own diagnostics are stronger. Since the lab excludes SHD, PS-MNIST and sequential CIFAR (pixel-level, 1,024 steps) plus GSC are the expected names.
- The "where does the gain come from" question appears in three forms that all apply to a two-path model: parameter/compute control (CPG-PE), separation of the new mechanism from a non-spiking or borrowed sub-module (P-SpikeSSM), and separation of components (ICLR 2026 submissions). For the lab this means: equal-parameter memory-less control, sequential path alone, attention path alone, and attention path with PE.
- No review I could read asked for an input-shuffle control in an SNN paper, while time-series reviewers at ICLR 2026 know the "permute-only-at-inference" pitfall. A shuffle-retrain plus memory-less package would exceed current SNN practice rather than merely meet it; it is not yet something reviewers demand in this sub-field.

- No public reviews exist for TC-LIF and SpikingSSM (AAAI) or SeqSNN (ICML 2024); the Nature-family peer-review files for DH-SNN and the dual-memory-pathway paper were not opened.
- SpikingSSM (AAAI 2025) was downloaded but not re-read; the earlier internal notes describe its LIF as receiving the SSM output.
- Review content is second-hand paraphrase (see reading conventions); ratings may be pre-rebuttal values.

## 5. Semi-synthetic constructions that guarantee an order / memory requirement while staying on real signals

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed precedents exist for four constructions: real content followed by a long distractor (noise-padded CIFAR), delayed comparison of two cue segments (delayed spiking XOR), arithmetic over a long stream (adding), and order-recognition pretext tasks on real EEG and wearable signals (temporal shuffling, relative positioning, permutation / time-reversal detection). They are accepted as mechanism probes when paired with real benchmarks and are not accepted alone as evidence of practical value.

### Cited Findings

- Noise-padded CIFAR-10: each image row is one input step; "after the first 32 time steps, we input independent standard Gaussian noise for the remaining time steps", total T = 1000, so "only the first 32 time steps of input contain salient information, all remaining 968 time steps are merely random noise ... it has to remember the information from a long time ago"; LSTM "fails to train at all" (Sec. 5, Table 2). — [Chang et al., AntisymmetricRNN, ICLR 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09689). Reused as a standard long-dependency test in [coRNN, ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00951) and [LEM, ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04744).
- Delayed spiking XOR and multi-timescale XOR: two rate-coded patterns separated by a noisy delay; the label is the XOR of the two; in the multi-timescale version every query must be XOR-ed with the first signal (Fig. 3–4, Methods). — [DH-SNN](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10766638/). Reused at ICLR 2025 in [TS-LIF App. A.11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05108), where it answered a reviewer's doubt about long-term dependency evidence ([forum rDe9yQQYKt](https://openreview.net/forum?id=rDe9yQQYKt), review paraphrase).
- Adding problem at long lengths as the first experiment of recurrent-model papers ("very long adding problem", Sec. 4 of LEM; Sec. 4 of coRNN), and a binary adding task with lengths 100–2400 as one of three validated SNN temporal benchmarks (Sec. IV–V, Fig. 5 and 8). — [LEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04744); [coRNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00951); [Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09449)
- Synthetic ordered-action task inside a real-task benchmark: NSA's "AL" asks whether a simulated robot ends on the left or right half-plane after "a sequence of ordered actions" (turn left, turn right, go straight, stop), with user-defined length (Sec. 2.2). — [NSA, IJCAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22035)
- Order-recognition tasks on real biosignals, used as self-supervised pretext tasks: "relative positioning" (are two EEG windows close in time) and "temporal shuffling" (is a triplet of EEG windows in order) on clinical EEG. — [Banville et al., J. Neural Eng. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.16104). Binary "was this transformation applied" tasks on accelerometer signals, including Permuted (slices within a temporal window reordered) and Horizontally Flipped (time reversal). — [Saeed et al., IMWUT 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11879)
- Removing rate cues from real spiking speech so that only timing can solve the task (rate-normalised SHD/SSC variants), with time-reversal as probe. — [Yu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16043)
- Reception. The dual-benchmark logic is stated by the SNN benchmark authors themselves: existing sets "do not adequately evaluate" temporal processing, hence constructed tasks with adjustable length next to real ones. — [Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09449). Among Swelam et al.'s criticisms of UCR is that 52 of 128 datasets have artificially constructed labels that are "best understood as pretext tasks" and should not be reported as if they were real prediction problems (Sec. 3.1). — [Swelam et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09843)

### Inferences
- A delayed-cue or delayed-match task built on real ECG or audio segments has direct precedent in structure (noise-padded CIFAR = real content + distractor delay; delayed XOR = two cues + delay) and in the type of signal (pretext tasks on real EEG / IMU). I found no peer-reviewed paper that uses "real physiological segments as cues with a real-signal delay" as an evaluation benchmark; the lab's construction would be new in that combination, and should be described as an adaptation of noise-padded CIFAR and delayed XOR.
- To be read as an order task rather than only a memory task, the label must change when the cue order is swapped (temporal-order judgement, or a non-commutative rule), because XOR and adding are commutative: they need accumulation, not order. The temporal-shuffling pretext task and time-reversal detection are order tasks by construction.
- The Swelam et al. criticism marks the boundary reviewers are likely to draw: a constructed label is acceptable as a diagnostic that is called a diagnostic, and becomes a weakness when presented as an application result.
- Parameters that reviewers have seen and accepted: delay swept until the baseline neuron reaches chance (TS-LIF Table 11: LIF at chance from delay 20), sequence length swept until models fail (binary adding to 2400), distractor length fixed at about 30 times the content length (noise-padded CIFAR: 32 content steps, 968 noise steps).

### Gaps
- No precedent found for concatenated-recording tasks on non-spiking physiological signals (e.g. two ECG excerpts whose order defines the label). An "adding" variant built from concatenated SHD digits is, to my memory, used in the SNN delay-learning literature, but I did not verify the source and SHD is excluded by the lab.
- I found no public review that criticises a delayed-XOR-type task as a toy; equally, no review praises one beyond accepting it as an answer. Reception evidence is therefore thin and indirect.
- Neuroscience-style tasks (store-recall, evidence accumulation, delayed match-to-sample in LSNN / e-prop papers) were not re-read in this session.

## 6. A recommended evidence package, with precedents for the pattern

### Takeaway
The pattern with the most precedent is: validate each task with model-independent controls first, then one constructed task where the requirement holds by design and difficulty is swept, then two or three real tasks that passed the validation, then standard benchmarks showing no loss, with component and parameter-matched ablations throughout. Recurrent-model papers at ICLR/ICML/NeurIPS and the main SNN neuron papers all follow some version of it.

### Cited Findings (precedents for each element)

- Validate the benchmark before comparing models: STP is applied to 12 common benchmarks and to the proposed tasks, and only tasks with STBP > SDBP > NoTD are kept as temporal benchmarks (Sec. 3.1, Table 2, Tables S2–S4). — [NSA, IJCAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22035); [Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09449). Dataset papers validate themselves with an order-free baseline: count-only SVM versus temporal models. — [Cramer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07407). Benchmark choice by shuffle-retrain: Something-Something (large ordered-versus-shuffled gap) versus UCF101 (none). — [TRN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08496)
- Constructed task first, then real tasks: delayed XOR → SHD/SSC → S-/PS-MNIST and others. — [DH-SNN](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10766638/). Very long adding → sMNIST/psMNIST → noisy CIFAR-10 → EigenWorms → heart-rate prediction from PPG → Google12 keyword spotting. — [LEM, ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04744). Adding → (p)sMNIST → noise-padded CIFAR-10 → HAR-2 → IMDB. — [coRNN, ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00951). Selective Copying and Induction Heads → language, audio, genomics. — [Mamba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752). Formal-language state-tracking tasks → associative recall → LRA → language modelling. — [xLSTM, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04517)
- Dataset-level diagnostic inside a model paper: last-k versus random-k training on EigenWorms to argue the dataset has very long dependencies (Fig. 3). — [UnICORNN, ICML 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05487)
- Controls that separate mechanism from capacity: Random-PE of equal size (Table 2). — [CPG-PE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14362). Component removal and comparison with SSM / spiking-SSM variants at stated parameter counts (Extended Data Table 2). — [Sun et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07602). Reset removal (Table 1). — [PSN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12760)
- What reviewers add when an element is missing: an isolating task for long-term dependency (TS-LIF), a harder sequence benchmark than PS-MNIST and attribution of the gain to the claimed component (P-SpikeSSM), PS-MNIST / sequential CIFAR for any new neuron (ICLR 2026) — review paraphrases. — [forum rDe9yQQYKt](https://openreview.net/forum?id=rDe9yQQYKt); [forum Sf4ep9Udjf](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Sf4ep9Udjf); [forum 9W38INOZ00](https://openreview.net/forum?id=9W38INOZ00)
- Retraining as the accepted form of an information-removal claim. — [ROAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10758); [O'Connor & Andreas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08367); review paraphrase crediting train-and-test-consistent permutation: [forum 8vgP9VhECt](https://openreview.net/forum?id=8vgP9VhECt)

### Inferences (the package, as a proposal derived from the findings above)

1. Task-validation table, one row per task, model-independent as far as possible. Columns: (a) truncation curve, last-k and random-k; (b) per-sample shuffle with retraining, several seeds, plus block-shuffle at increasing block size to locate the scale at which order matters; (c) order-free baseline given the same tokens (mean-pooled token classifier or attention without PE and with a pooling head, checked to be order-blind by an exact permutation test of its outputs); (d) memory-less control of the sequential path. A task enters the main results only if (b) and (c) show a loss. State in the text that test-time shuffling is reported separately as a model-behaviour analysis, citing ROAR / O'Connor & Andreas for the distinction.
2. One constructed task on real segments, in two variants so that order and accumulation are shown separately: a commutative variant (delayed XOR / match, needs memory only) and an order variant (label flips when the two cue segments are swapped). Sweep the delay until the order-blind and memory-less controls reach chance, as in TS-LIF Table 11 and the binary adding sweep. Present it explicitly as a diagnostic adapted from noise-padded CIFAR and delayed spiking XOR.
3. Two or three real tasks that pass step 1. Candidates with published memory-less evidence and no conflict with the lab's exclusions: Google Speech Commands, WISDM HAR (length 200), OpenBMI EEG motor imagery (length 500), PS-MNIST / pixel-level sequential CIFAR as the names reviewers expect. Candidates that are order-defined by label semantics but lack published diagnostics: rhythm classes defined by beat order (bigeminy, trigeminy, Wenckebach). EigenWorms should be expected to behave as an evidence-accumulation task given the random-k result.
4. Standard benchmarks (ETT, MIT-BIH) reported as "no loss", with the validation table showing that they do not reward order beyond what the head already provides; this turns the current null result into a documented property of the benchmarks rather than a weakness of the model.
5. Attribution of the gain: parameter-matched memory-less control, sequential path alone, attention path alone, attention path with a positional encoding. The last one matters most: if attention with PE closes the gap on the real tasks, the supported claim is "order information without a positional encoding, at lower cost", not "sequential memory is required".
6. Supplementary only: gradient-through-time or reach plots and state-decoding analyses; the literature shows saliency over time is unreliable as primary evidence.

### Gaps
- None of the precedents combines all elements; in particular no SNN paper I read reports a shuffle-retrain task-validation table, so there is no direct evidence of how reviewers receive one in this sub-field.
- I found no precedent in which a paper's main contribution was accepted on the strength of a constructed task plus real tasks where the real-task gain was small; the size of real-task gain reviewers consider sufficient is not documented in what I could read.
- Whether Google Speech Commands, WISDM or OpenBMI still favour a sequential path when an attention block sees all tokens is not answered by the published memory-less probes (their backbones had no attention).
